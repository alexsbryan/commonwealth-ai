# Governance

Today it's one steward. The aim is a constitution, not a throne — and yes, this is the throne guy writing the constitution. First draft.

## A commons, governed

Commonwealth is a commons: people pooling machines and knowledge for shared work. The old story says a commons ends in tragedy. It's mostly wrong — turns out people are pretty good at sharing a field when you let them make the rules. That's the bet.

You can see it in the software already. A mesh has edges: in by invitation, out whenever. Nothing's shared unless both sides say so. No outside party can overrule the group. A hundred meshes, a hundred ways, no center. None of that's decoration — it's how good commons have always run, baked into the architecture instead of a promise you'd have to take on faith. The governance is just trying to catch up to what the product already pulls off.

## Where it is now

One steward. I hold the vision and the architecture and make the final call. (It's me. Hi.)

That's not the opposite of the aim, it's how you get there. You don't hand a newborn commons a finished constitution and wish it luck. You keep the thing coherent long enough for real rules to form, write them down, then live under them. A constitution worth the name binds the person who wrote it. Steering closely now is how I earn the right to stop.

I've worked on big teams. Aligning people you see every day is hard enough; strangers on the internet, harder. So the circle stays small for a bit — chosen like a band, not an open mic. A phase, and I'm calling it one on purpose so nobody mistakes it for a personality.

## How it opens

The people under a rule should, in time, be the ones making it. The way in is earned, and open to anyone willing:

- Show up — issues, discussions, tales from running a real mesh.
- Earn some trust, get pulled onto the team, with real say over your corner.
- As the ground firms up, more decisions move into the open and the steward's power quietly shrinks — written down here as it goes.

Source opens with the beta. Alpha collaborators have it now; the signup's how you knock.

## Deciding, for now

Informed by you, decided by me — with "for now" doing a lot of honest work in that sentence. A thumbs-up is a vote. Each cycle I take what's loudest, pick what I can actually carry, and say why. Votes steer the roadmap; they don't drive it yet. That "yet" is the whole plan.

I'll make calls you disagree with. Fair — worth taking seriously, and the answer's some mix of listening, explaining, changing my mind, and occasionally just holding the line. I'll be wrong sometimes. I'd rather hear about it and fix it than get attached.

## Who it answers to

The people who use it for the work it's for come first — not funders, not anyone who never has to live with the call. When interests collide, users win.

And if it ever drifts somewhere you can't follow, the door's unlocked: fork it, it's AGPL, no hard feelings. I hope you won't need to.

Mostly, though, this is one person trying to make something genuinely cool for as many people as possible, out in the open, and having a good time doing it. It'll get to be more than I can hold alone — everything good does — and when it does I'll say so and ask for help, right here, no drama. A little grace for whoever's keeping the thing running goes a long way, and I'll try to earn it.

This'll change — a constitution you can't amend isn't one, it's just a mood. Call it a first draft, from someone whose whole job is to make the next drafts need him less.

Anyway. Be patient, be constructive, and when this thing really sings, demand the best.
