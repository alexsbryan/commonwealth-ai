# Catalogue entry 1 — Aldfrith penny, Series Y

A silver sceatta of Aldfrith, king of Northumbria, struck at the Eoforwic
mint. Obverse: a stylised lion walking left, tail raised over the back.
Reverse: a pellet-in-annulet within a beaded border. Weight 1.21 g,
diameter 12 mm, die axis 180 degrees. The issue is conventionally placed
in the reign of Aldfrith, that is between 685 and 704, though the narrower
dating within that reign is contested (see Halstead 2014 and Ferreira 2021
below). Two specimens from the Wessex Down hoard share an obverse die.

# Catalogue entry 2 — Series R sceatta, runic type

A silver sceatta of the Series R group, struck at the Gipeswic mint. The
obverse carries a radiate bust right with the runic legend EPA; the reverse
shows a standard with four pellets. Weight 0.95 g, diameter 11 mm. No ruler
is named on the coin and none can be inferred from the types; Series R is a
mint issue rather than a royal one. The Wessex Down hoard contained
fourteen examples, of which four are die-duplicates.

# Catalogue entry 3 — Beonna penny

A silver penny of Beonna, king of the East Angles, struck at the Gipeswic
mint by the moneyer Efe. Obverse: the runic legend BEONNA REX around a
central pellet. Reverse: the moneyer's name in a cross-and-pellets field.
Weight 1.05 g, diameter 13 mm. Beonna reigned from about 749; the coinage
is metrologically distinct from the sceattas that precede it, being struck
to a heavier standard and to a markedly higher silver fineness.

# Catalogue entry 4 — Offa gold dinar

A gold imitation dinar of Offa, king of Mercia, struck at the Lundenwic
mint. The obverse copies an Abbasid Arabic legend, blundered, with the
Latin legend OFFA REX inserted upside down relative to the Arabic. The
reverse continues the Arabic. Weight 4.28 g, diameter 20 mm. The prototype
is dated by its own Arabic legend to AH 157, that is 773 or 774, which
gives the Mercian copy a terminus post quem. Offa reigned 757 to 796.

# Catalogue entry 5 — Coenwulf mancus

A gold mancus of Coenwulf, king of Mercia, struck at the Lundenwic mint by
the moneyer Deigmund. Obverse: a diademed bust right, legend COENVVLF REX
MERCIORVM. Reverse: DE VICO LVNDONIAE in four lines. Weight 4.33 g,
diameter 21 mm. Only one specimen is known outside the Wessex Down hoard.

# Catalogue entry 6 — Series X sceatta, "Wodan/monster" type

A silver sceatta of the Series X group. The obverse shows a facing head
with radiating hair; the reverse a fantastic beast looking back over its
shoulder. Weight 1.14 g, diameter 12 mm. No ruler's name appears. Series X
is usually assigned to a mint at Ribe rather than to any English mint, and
its presence in the Wessex Down hoard is evidence of North Sea circulation
rather than of local striking.

# Catalogue entry 7 — billon sceatta, Series E "porcupine"

A billon sceatta of the Series E group, degraded standard, struck at an
unidentified continental mint. Obverse: the so-called porcupine, a curved
line of pellets over a crescent. Reverse: a standard with the letters TOT
II. Weight 0.88 g, diameter 11 mm. The silver content is visibly low; the
flan is coppery where it has worn.

# The mint at Eoforwic

Eoforwic, the Northumbrian mint at York, struck the royal sceatta coinage
of the later seventh and eighth centuries. Its output is distinguished from
the southern mints by the consistent use of an animal obverse rather than a
bust, and by a slightly heavier weight standard.

# The mint at Gipeswic

Gipeswic, at Ipswich in East Anglia, was the most productive of the English
sceatta mints. It struck the Series R group over several decades and, later,
the reformed penny coinage of Beonna. Moneyers' names appear on the Beonna
issues but not on the earlier Series R.

# The mint at Lundenwic

Lundenwic, the trading settlement west of the Roman walls of London, struck
for the Mercian kings from the eighth century. Both the Offa gold dinar and
the Coenwulf mancus name it in their reverse legends.

# Aldfrith of Northumbria

Aldfrith ruled Northumbria from 685 until his death in 704. He was the
first English king to place his own name on a coinage, and the Series Y
sceattas of Eoforwic are that coinage.

# Offa of Mercia

Offa ruled Mercia from 757 until 796 and was the dominant English king of
the later eighth century. His coinage includes the reformed broad penny
and, exceptionally, the gold dinar imitation catalogued above.

# Coenwulf of Mercia

Coenwulf ruled Mercia from 796 until 821, succeeding Offa's short-lived
son. His gold mancus is the only English gold coin of the period naming
both a king and a mint.

# Beonna of East Anglia

Beonna ruled the East Angles from about 749. His penny coinage is the
earliest English coinage struck to a deliberately reformed weight and
fineness standard.

# Halstead 2014 — the die-link case for an early Aldfrith issue

Halstead argues that the Series Y sceattas of Aldfrith belong to the first
years of the reign, between 685 and 690. The argument is a die-link: the
obverse die of the Wessex Down specimens is shared with a coin from the
Ferrybridge find, and that coin was struck over an issue of Aldfrith's
predecessor whose own terminal date is fixed. The chain of dies is
therefore anchored, and the anchor sits early in the reign.

# Ferreira 2021 — the hoard context of the Aldfrith penny

Ferreira dates the same Series Y sceattas of Aldfrith to the last decade of
the reign, between 695 and 704. The argument is hoard context: in the
Wessex Down deposit the Aldfrith pennies show markedly less wear than the
Series R coins buried with them, and the Series R group is independently
dated no earlier than about 710. A coin struck in 685 could not be fresher
than a coin struck in 710. Ferreira states plainly that Halstead's die
chain must be reversed in direction.

# Nkemdirim 2018 — weight standards of the Beonna coinage

Nkemdirim dates the Beonna pennies of the moneyer Efe to the last years of
the reign, between 757 and 760. The argument is metrological: the surviving
specimens cluster at 1.05 g, a standard reached only after the earlier and
lighter Efe dies had been withdrawn, and the weight series is monotonic
across the reign.

# Vasquez 2020 — the style of the Series R runic dies

Vasquez dates the runic Series R dies to between 710 and 725 on stylistic
grounds. The radiate bust is copied from a Merovingian prototype whose own
currency is confined to those years, and the runic EPA legend is cut by a
hand that does not appear on any later Gipeswic die.

# Ferreira 2021 — Series R in the Wessex Down hoard

Ferreira places the Series R group between 715 and 740 on hoard context.
The Wessex Down deposit contains Series R coins in three distinct wear
states, which argues for a long period of issue rather than a short one.
This is a widening of the conventional dating rather than a challenge to
it, and Ferreira notes agreement with Vasquez on the early end.

# Halstead 2014 and Nkemdirim 2018 on the Coenwulf mancus

Both Halstead and Nkemdirim date the Coenwulf mancus to between 805 and
810, and for the same reason: the moneyer Deigmund is attested in the
Lundenwic charter witness lists only in those years. The two attributions
agree in date, in evidence and in grade.
