A small group of friends decides to plan a weekend hike together. They study a topographic map of the area, marking the trailhead, three rest points, and the summit they hope to reach by mid-afternoon. The youngest member, who has never hiked at this altitude before, asks how long the climb will take. The most experienced member explains that pace depends on terrain, weight in their packs, and how often the group stops to drink water and eat. They estimate roughly four hours up and three hours down, with a generous lunch at the summit. The group also discusses what to do if the weather turns: where to seek shelter, how to recognize early signs of a thunderstorm, and which trails offer faster routes back to the car. They agree to leave a written plan with a friend in town, including their expected return time and a list of people to call if they are not back by sunset. After packing layered clothing, two liters of water each, energy bars, and a small first-aid kit, the friends turn off their phones and head outside to load the trailhead permit into the windshield of the car.

In light of the plan above, briefly summarize what makes group hiking safer than hiking alone, and propose two additional precautions the friends could take.
