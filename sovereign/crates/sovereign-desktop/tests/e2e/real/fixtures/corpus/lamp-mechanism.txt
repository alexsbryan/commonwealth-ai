# The Lamp and Rotation Mechanism

The Meridian Lighthouse originally burned colza oil in a four-wick
concentric burner, upgraded to kerosene in 1894. The station was
electrified in 1921, among the first on the coast, using a submarine
cable laid across Hollow Sound.

The optic is a second-order Fresnel lens manufactured by the Aubert
works in 1886. It weighs roughly 2.8 tonnes and floats on a mercury
bath bearing, which allows the entire lens assembly to be rotated by
hand with a single finger.

Rotation was driven by a clockwork mechanism: a 220-kilogram weight
descended through the tower's central column and had to be rewound by
the keeper every two and a half hours. The clockwork was retired at
electrification in 1921, replaced by a quarter-horsepower motor, but
the original mechanism remains in place and is demonstrated monthly
by museum volunteers.
