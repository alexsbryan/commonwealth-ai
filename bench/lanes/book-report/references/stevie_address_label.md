## Passage A — Heat reveals the address cloth (lines 3838-3867)

> "I've brought away an address," he said, pulling out of his pocket
> without haste a singed rag of dark blue cloth. "This belongs to the
> overcoat the fellow who got himself blown to pieces was wearing. Of
> course, the overcoat may not have been his, and may even have been
> stolen. But that's not at all probable if you look at this."
>
> The Chief Inspector, stepping up to the table, smoothed out carefully
> the rag of blue cloth. He had picked it up from the repulsive heap in
> the mortuary, because a tailor's name is found sometimes under the
> collar. It is not often of much use, but still—He only half expected
> to find anything useful, but certainly he did not expect to find—not
> under the collar at all, but stitched carefully on the under side of
> the lapel—a square piece of calico with an address written on it in
> marking ink.
>
> [...]
>
> Only the number 32 and the name of Brett Street were written in
> marking ink on a piece of calico slightly larger than an ordinary
> cigarette paper.

**Key detail:** *under side of the lapel* (not collar); *32 Brett
Street* (the Verloc shop address); *marking ink* on calico.

## Passage B — Heat doesn't surrender the evidence (lines 4526-4528)

> The piece of overcoat with the address sewn on was certainly not a
> thing to leave about.

**Key detail:** Heat keeps the cloth — withholds it from the Assistant
Commissioner. Plot consequence: the investigation almost goes wrong.

## Passage C — Verloc realizes what happened (lines 6415-6418)

> "Purple—isn't it?" he remarked, setting it down again. "As I said,
> it's strange. Because the overcoat has got a label sewn on the inside
> with your address written in marking ink."

**Key detail:** Heat tells Verloc the address is *his own*. This is
the moment Verloc understands Stevie was the bomber. Winnie sewed
the label in her brother's coat as a precaution — her act of care
becomes the chain of evidence.

## What an A-grade answer needs

1. Names the **address label / calico patch with address in marking ink**
2. Locates it on **the under side of the lapel** (not just "the coat")
3. Names **Winnie** as the one who sewed it in
4. Notes the **dramatic irony** — her protective act unravels the plot

## What gets docked

- Saying "a tag" or "a note" without specifying the address
- Saying "in his pocket" or "on the collar" (the text is specific)
- Attributing the label to Verloc or to Stevie himself
- Hallucinating a *quoted passage* that doesn't appear in pg974.txt
