# Design pitches and seat recommendations must lead with numbers and falsifiable predictions — the operator does not act on prima facie…

Operator process note (2026-08-08, during the H2→H2b redesign pitch): "If you can't provide numbers and start convincing me analytically then I'm really not going to be able to provide convicted direction. The fact is I don't just trust an opinion prima facie."

Why: a pitch built on architectural elegance ("the machinery exists", "it collapses two mechanisms into one") gives the operator nothing to push back on. Numbers, measured bases, quantified predictions, and pre-stated kill bars are what let them steer with conviction — same discipline ARCH §18.5 demands of results, applied to PROPOSALS.

How to apply: every seat pitch/design recommendation carries: (1) the measured numbers it builds on, cited to their artifacts; (2) falsifiable predictions with rough magnitudes and honest bands; (3) a quantified kill criterion decided before the work runs; (4) the cost model with measured per-unit bases, not vibes. If a quantity is unknown, name it as the thing the first deliverable measures. See [[feedback_report_actual_metric_comparisons]] and [[feedback_journey_first_design]].

Refinement (operator, same day, after the Goodhart-premise falsification): "It's just about having a little more of an approach of critical thinking — not exactly so that you can spit the whole process at me, but more the results of some high discernment." The checking happens UPSTREAM and silently — read the artifact/code BEFORE forming the opinion (the seat pitched a topic-inflation risk in H1's calibration set without reading calibration.rs, which documented same-article-hard construction in its own comments; a worker falsified it in one artifact read). What reaches the operator is the RESULT of that discernment at its compressed weight — one verified sentence beats a risk pitch plus a falsification cycle. Not analysis volume; pre-verified conclusions.
