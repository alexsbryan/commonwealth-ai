MESH FAIRNESS PRINCIPLE — normative, stated by the project owner 2026-07-26. Recorded in sovereign/docs/specs/SCHEDULER_QUALITY.md §F6, which SUPERSEDES the framing in MESH_INFERENCE.md §1.

"Floors are equal; surplus may be earned." Under contention, mesh capacity is divided max-min fairly across origins REGARDLESS of contribution. Contribution can only influence access to capacity nobody else is currently asking for — uncontended capacity, by definition, takes nothing from anyone.

WHY (owner's words, paraphrased): the mesh must not concentrate. A node that CANNOT afford to contribute — old laptop, metered link, under-resourced region — must not be crowded out by nodes that already hold the resources and want to use all of them. The objection to contribution-weighting is NOT primarily anti-commodification (which is how MESH_INFERENCE.md §1 read it); it is anti-CONCENTRATION. That distinction changes the design.

THE MECHANICAL ERROR TO AVOID — a cap floor is NOT a service floor:
- effective_peer_cap (commonwealth-api/src/state.rs:377) gives a neutral node PEER_BASE_CAP = 1 and a top contributor the whole ceiling. That "1" bounds YOUR OWN concurrency; it RESERVES NOTHING. When held == slots_total, try_grant (commonwealth-core/src/fair_sched.rs:329) queues or sheds regardless of who you are.
- Contribution compounds in TWO places: cap size AND queue order. best_eligible ranks waiters by ranks_ahead(weight, seq) (fair_sched.rs:243), FIFO only as a tiebreak WITHIN equal weight — so a low-weight waiter is passed by every high-weight arrival, indefinitely.

THE INVERSION THAT MAKES IT WORSE (the concentration loop, stated mechanically): a node with a weak local model is exactly the node for which peers most easily "strictly beat local" (peer_inference.rs:1129), so it DEPENDS on the mesh most. It is also the node with the least to contribute — smallest cap, last in line. The mechanism sheds hardest exactly where dependency is highest.

THE TWO CHANGES: (1) queue order stops ranking by contribution — replace `weight` in ranks_ahead with a SERVICE DEFICIT (how little this origin was served in a recent window). Deficit round robin / max-min fair share, a policy with a well-defined optimum, which is what gives the bench an unambiguous target. (2) reciprocity weight stops scaling effective_peer_cap under contention and instead governs who may exceed the equal share when slots are otherwise IDLE. This also resolves the ledger-charter question: the ledger never decides WHETHER you are served, only who mops up slack.

BENCH OBLIGATIONS (in SCHEDULER_QUALITY.md §5 scoreboard): worst-served-origin share + Jain's index under sustained contention; floor-violation events (an origin below its equal share while another exceeded its own); DEPENDENCY-WEIGHTED SHED RATE (shed rate conditioned on how much worse the origin's local option was) — the concentration-loop detector, drives to zero in a healthy mesh.

CURRENTLY INERT IN PRODUCTION: DEFAULT_PEER_INFLIGHT_CEILING = usize::MAX (state.rs:360), so effective_peer_cap returns u32::MAX and no rationing happens at all. That is the OPPORTUNITY — the fairness policy can be designed and bench-proven before it ever gates a real request. DO NOT switch on a finite ceiling before the floor metrics exist.
