THREE FINDINGS THAT WOULD BE EXPENSIVE TO RE-DERIVE:

1. THE SINGLE-SEED-ACT DRAW DOES NOT CLOSE. PRIMITIVES.md's original sketch seeded sortition from "the first act after a boundary not authored by the drawer". An ActId is sha256 of its own body, so whoever writes that act grinds bodies until the shuffle favours them — a few hundred tries for a pool of 20. The race to be first is NOT a defence (attacker writes at after_ts+1). What shipped is commit-reveal across the pool (`draw_commit`/`draw_secret`/`draw_reveal`), and THE DRAW IS A QUERY, NOT AN ACT — nobody performs it, so there is nothing to perform badly. Named residual: the last revealer has one bit (reveal vs stay silent), costing them their own seat. Standard for commit-reveal without an external beacon; does not close with a ledger alone.

2. `grant`/`withdraw` body field is `holder`, NEVER `actor`. ActKind is `#[serde(flatten)]`ed into the Act envelope, which already has `actor`. A body field of that name emits a line with two `actor` keys — writable once, unparseable forever after. Found by running the CLI, not the suite. The gate that was missing now exists: `every_act_kind_survives_the_wire_with_its_id_intact` in canon-core/src/tests.rs, whose coverage count is DERIVED from STRUCTURAL + KNOWN_ANNOTATIONS so a new op fails it until it has a round-trip instance. Watched it fail on the original bug.

3. GRANTS CLOSE WITH A DATE, THEY ARE NEVER DELETED. The fold used to `retain()` a withdrawn grant away, which meant a withdrawal today silently shrank a draw pool frozen last week. `Grant.withdrawn_at` + `Grant::held_at(now)` make `who_decides` a genuine as-of query. `who_decides` also dedups by PERSON (narrowest standing kept) — holding both `house` and `house.kitchen` is one decider, not two, and quorum would have been counted off that list.

DESIGN DEVIATIONS FROM THE PLAN, deliberate: policy ships as one serializable `Rule` enum (closed set, lives in the ledger) with `Policy` kept as the trait extension point, rather than four structs; `decide` returns a `Decision{outcome,authority,because}` not a tuple, because the bar has to assert WHICH rule fired; `check.rs` collapsed TWO outcome matches into one Voice table, not three — `render_personal` never matched on outcome (it renders stakes, never a verdict) and stays its own function.
