WHAT WAS BROKEN (pre-2026-08-07):
1. `read_get_chunk` fails → the ~200-char `snippet` was pushed into the pool indistinguishably from a stored body. The grounding oracle then judged 200 chars while the model had read up to MAX_CHUNK_CHARS — the ORACLE seeing LESS than the system, the opposite direction from truncation, surfacing only as an impossible >100% delivery ratio.
2. No snippet either → the chunk was dropped from the pool entirely, `resolved` under-counting with nothing recorded.
3. Degradation was INFERRED downstream as `promptText.length > text.length`. That heuristic misses every case where the substituted snippet is longer than the delivered body, and misses dropped chunks completely.
All three were invisible because the failure path was a bare `catch {}`.

THE INVARIANT: arrays returned by the resolver are aligned 1:1 with the RETRIEVED chunk list — an unresolvable position holds "" with `resolution[i] = "missing"`, never a hole. `pool` is the compacted, non-empty view and is byte-identical to the pre-fix `texts`, which is what keeps `resolved`/`chars`/`text` comparable against every journal already on disk. Read `deliveredKnown` before reading ANY delivery ratio, and DISCARD rows with `resolutionDegraded > 0` before deriving a decline taxonomy — a "not in the evidence" verdict is unsafe on those.
