THE 4B NEEDS A GENUINE 48GB CARD — 44-45GB IS MEASURED TO ABORT. VRAM floor is 46, and 44 was tried and FALSIFIED. 2026-08-05.

THE FALSIFYING RUN. RTX A6000 reading 44.43GB, Vast 46857925, $0.4028/hr. Passed preflight at floor 44 (margin 0.43GB) and then ABORTED AT STEP 4 OF 25 on the memory tripwire:
  step 2 111.87s/it -> WARN cuda-mem-get-info 41.0GB over --mem-limit-gb 40.9 (1 consecutive)
  step 3  75.46s/it -> WARN (2 consecutive)
  step 4          -> ABORT on 3 CONSECUTIVE samples
Memory at abort: allocated peak 35.89GB, reserved 40.49GB, device-wide 40.96GB against a 40.9GB tripwire (92% of 44.43).

THE LESSON, which is the whole reason the floor moved twice: DEMAND IS NOT THE ALLOCATED PEAK. It is allocated + the caching allocator's reserve + the CUDA context. Allocated was 35.89 on the A6000 and 36.53 on the A100 — nearly identical and both comfortably under 44 — while real device-wide demand is ~41GB. Sizing a card from `gpu_peak_gb` alone underestimates by ~5GB and picks a card that cannot finish.

FLOOR HISTORY, all three values evidenced:
  52 — from the Halo's 51.88GB peak, but that was measured on OVER-LENGTH sequences (6410 vs 4096) and was an overestimate. It rejected the entire $0.39-0.54/hr tier.
  44 — from the A100's 36.53GB allocated peak + headroom. MY PREDICTION, FALSIFIED by the run above.
  46 — refuses the 44-45GB readings that demonstrably abort; admits the 47.37-48.0GB readings (RTX 6000 Ada / A6000 48GB / RTX PRO 5000), where a 92% tripwire sits at ~44GB with ~3GB over the measured 41GB demand. STILL A PREDICTION for 46-48; the only COMPLETED runs are the A100 (80GB) and this abort.

THE TRIPWIRE DID ITS JOB (§18.3). It stopped cleanly, so the adapter, steps.jsonl and timings survived — "an OOM SIGKILL would take both". This is the CUDA branch that was silently inert before 2026-08-04. Note that it aborts at 92% of DEVICE total, so it fires on a co-tenant's memory too; on a dedicated pod that is the intended conservatism.

SET `--vram-floor-gb` IN ONE PLACE. `pod.sh`'s search query and `preflight.py` are now one `VRAM_FLOOR_GB`, exported across the ssh boundary to `provision.sh`. Before this they were 60 and 52 independently, and the SEARCH one silently dominated — preflight could never fail a card the search had already refused to rent.
