**F7 — `cold_start_weight`'s doc comment states a normative claim that the code does not honour: the ramp is SELF-LOCKING.** (Measured 2026-07-26 by the Tier-1 mesh-sim running the real scorer.)

THE CLAIM, in `oicp-types/src/scoring.rs:291-296`: the ramp exists "so new peers still receive routable traffic (otherwise they'd never accumulate history)".

WHY IT IS FALSE. `samples` increments only on dispatch (`MeshInferenceProvider::record_dispatch` → `scheduler_core::observe_dispatch`). `cold_start_weight` multiplies a cold peer's score by COLD_START_MIN_WEIGHT (0.7) while the LOCAL candidate carries `locality_bonus` 1.15 vs a LAN peer's 1.05 AND is seeded above the threshold at construction (`with_peer_source` sets local samples = COLD_START_SAMPLES * 2). So a cold peer needs ≈1.6× the local claim score merely to break even. Below that: never chosen → never sampled → never un-penalised. It is a ratchet, not a ramp.

MEASURED: household-evening-12 (12 nodes, 30 min, 128 decisions) — ONE node ever received a peer request; the other eleven received zero. Across household-evening-12 AND twin-hubs, NO (decider, peer) pair ever reached the 20 samples that complete the ramp. At household request volumes the ramp never completes for anyone, so it functions as a permanent flat 0.7–0.9 penalty on every peer and none on local.

HOW TO APPLY:
1. Do NOT cite the doc comment as evidence that new peers get traffic. They do not, at household volumes.
2. Any fix belongs in the sim as an arm first (SCHEDULER_QUALITY.md §6: behavioural work goes INTO the sim as arms, never into production first). Candidate arms: seed a new peer at the threshold rather than the floor; make the ramp time-based rather than sample-based; give a cold peer an exploration budget.
3. This compounds F6 from the other direction — F6 is who gets SERVED under contention, F7 is who ever gets TRIED. Both concentrate work on the incumbent, which is the outcome the mesh fairness principle (§F6, owner-stated) forbids.
4. It is also why F5's two-choices remedy is inert on a realistic fleet: the eligible set is a singleton because cold peers cannot clear local.
