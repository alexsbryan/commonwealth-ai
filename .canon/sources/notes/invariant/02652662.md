THE FLEET IS THE INSTRUMENT, NOT THE AUDIENCE. Operator framing correction 2026-08-06, and it retracted a recommendation I had already committed to the repo.

WHAT I DID WRONG: from M3-A (count = 0) and M3-B (mild x2.13) I recommended DROPPING M2 and M3, and argued MESH_N4_TOPOLOGY.md's exit clause was approaching. That reasoned from a measurement of THREE MACHINES WE HAPPEN TO OWN to a claim about a product meant to generalize across many deployments. The three boxes are the bench, not the market.

THE SPECIFIC TECHNICAL ERROR, which is the reusable part: M3-A requires "four nodes, each solo for the same model" — EVERY NODE IS A HOLDER. In that configuration local-first is nearly tautological and count=0 is unsurprising. But §4.5's consumer story — what the product is FOR — is THIN CLIENTS THAT HOLD NO MODEL. There every request is cross-node by construction, so the herd is not an edge case to dissolve, it is the STEADY STATE. A's zero says nothing about the shape that matters. A passing experiment in an unrepresentative configuration is not evidence about the representative one.

THEREFORE M3-B IS THE LOAD-BEARING EXPERIMENT AND IT WAS RUN AT TOY SCALE: 3 originators, 6 requests, a 0.8B, a ~27-token prompt, prefill negligible. The product shape is MANY thin originators -> ONE holder, real prompts, large model, where prefill dominates and x2.13 has no reason to hold. That measurement decides M2, M3 and M5. Not taken.

WHAT STILL GENERALIZES, because these are statements about MECHANISMS rather than about our hardware:
- C5: crossing a boundary costs ~71% of throughput at these link speeds -> tensor-splitting is a last resort; deprioritizes M4 on any fleet with a big-enough box.
- No prefix reuse on the chat path (note 9bfdef63): every deployment pays it, and THIN CLIENTS PAY HARDEST — they re-send context they cannot cache. This makes prefix reuse a product-level priority, not a local optimization.
- Today's named-path gate fixes (privacy, forward budget, refusal cause — commit 7021a11f) are LOAD-BEARING for the product rather than edge cases: a thin client holds nothing, so EVERY request is a forward and every one of those gates is on the hot path.

THE PATTERN TO WATCH IN MYSELF — three instances in one day, same shape: (1) C1, a stale-daemon crash read as a thin-peer load ceiling; (2) the 122B, a fit-gate reading promoted to a standing property; (3) this, our fleet read as the target deployment. Each time a LOCAL READING was generalized. Before concluding "X is true of the system", ask what population the observation was drawn from and whether it is the population the claim is about.

ALSO: the exit clause "not worth continuing if measurement shows one box serves THE REALISTIC FLEET" invites this error by naming a fleet instead of a target deployment. It needs re-scoping to the product's intended deployments before it is used to stop anything — objective-level, operator's call.
