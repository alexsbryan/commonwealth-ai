# THE DAEMON SHEDS WITH AN EMPTY QUEUE — 624 of 625 sheds had position=1. Root-caused 2026-08-26 on Alexs-MacBook-Pro-2 after a bench run's…

THE DAEMON SHEDS WITH AN EMPTY QUEUE — 624 of 625 sheds had `position=1`. Root-caused 2026-08-26 on Alexs-MacBook-Pro-2 after a bench run's 503s were nearly written off as "busy host". THEY WERE NOT LOAD. This is a production defect and it also silently corrupts the regression harness, because the same daemon IS the instrument.

=== THE MEASUREMENT (daemon.err, 10:39–15:28 UTC, ~5h) ===
625 `inference.queue: SHED` events.
  position=1  624   <-- the queue was EMPTY; one turn in flight, nobody parked
  position=2    1
  avg_turn_ms at shed: min 17312 / p50 30690 / p90 30690 / max 121875
  slot=lazy on all 625
The bound is `DEFAULT_MAX_QUEUE_WAIT_MS = 30_000`. p50 is 30,690 — the whole failure is a 2.3% overshoot.

=== THE MECHANISM, EXACTLY ===
`model_slot.rs`: on contention, `position = queue.depth() + 1`, so an EMPTY queue gives position 1.
`fair_sched.rs`: `predict_wait_ms(position, slots) = ceil(position/slots) * avg_turn_ms`, so position 1 / 1 slot = ONE FULL avg_turn_ms.
Shed when `predicted_wait_ms > max_wait_ms`.

Substitute: at position 1 the rule degenerates to **`avg_turn_ms > 30_000`**. It has NOTHING to do with load. The moment a host's smoothed turn exceeds 30s, effective concurrency drops to 1 and every concurrent caller gets a hard 503 — permanently, not transiently.

Two aggravations:
1. It charges the arriving caller a FULL avg_turn for the in-flight turn, ignoring elapsed time. A caller arriving at t=29s of a 31s turn is told it will wait 31s and is shed.
2. The EWMA is `avg = (dur + 3*avg) / 4`. One 8-minute synthesis (this host: 226k–484k ms per SEP synthesis) takes avg from ~31s to ~143s, and it needs ~7 SHORT turns to fall back under 30s. While it is above, only sequential turns can land — so if those are also syntheses the estimator never recovers.

`DEFAULT_MAX_QUEUE_WAIT_MS`'s own doc says the 30s "bets that a consumer who would wait half a minute prefers to know" and asks to be re-decided rather than re-derived. That reasoning is about CONGESTION. There is none here.

=== THE SECOND HALF: THE BACKPRESSURE IS PERFECT AND NOBODY ACTS ON IT ===
The daemon returns 503 + `Retry-After` + `{reason: "local_queue_full", retry_after_secs: 32}` — correctly, through one renderer (`admission::shed_response`), a fix earned by a prior fleet probe. No client retries. `oicp-client/src/lib.rs` has no retry loop at all (no `for attempt`, no `sleep`, no backoff); nothing under `sovereign/crates` sleeps on `retry_after_secs`. The hint is computed, serialised, transported, and dropped.

=== SELF-SHEDDING: ONE TURN REFUSES ITSELF ===
The log shows THREE requests inside 17 ms (decision_ids d00000480/481/482, each `served_by=failed`), all routed local, all shed. Since no retry loop exists, those are three DISTINCT sub-requests of one turn's own fan-out. So a single user turn, with no other client on the machine, gets its own sub-calls refused by its own host. That is the answer to "why would it 503 when nothing else is using it".

Incidental, same log lines: the router probes an unreachable peer on EVERY request — `peer manifest transport error — trying next peer=BeefyMac url=http://127.0.0.1:52334/oicp/v1/capabilities`. A stale peer entry costs a failed round-trip per inference call.

=== WHY THIS MATTERS BEYOND THE BENCH ===
A desktop user chatting while any long turn is in flight gets a hard 503, not a wait. And `scripts/sovereign-ci-bench.sh` measures ANSWER QUALITY against this daemon: on 2026-08-26 `synth:sep` reported FAIL(3reg) where 9 of 15 questions had merely been refused. The scoring half is fixed (note 933dccee — errors are excluded, not scored 0.0); THE REFUSALS STILL HAPPEN.

=== THE THREE CANDIDATE FIXES, NOT YET TAKEN (operator's call — the doc comment reserves the number) ===
1. Predict the REMAINING wait, not a full turn: subtract elapsed time on the in-flight turn. Correctness, not policy — position-1 sheds become rare and honest, real congestion (position>=2) still sheds.
2. Make the bound RELATIVE to the workload (`max(bound, k * avg_turn)`), so a big-model host does not shed on its own natural turn length. A fixed wall-clock number cannot be right for both a 4B and a 27B.
3. Honour `retry_after_secs` in the client on QueueShed only, bounded attempts and a total cap. Unambiguous: computing a hint nobody reads is waste, and this one is right there in the response.
Stopgap on a big-model host: `SOVEREIGN_MAX_QUEUE_WAIT_SECS` (0 disables the bound entirely).
