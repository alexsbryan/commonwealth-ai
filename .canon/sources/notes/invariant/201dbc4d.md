THE MEMORY TRIPWIRE FALSE-POSITIVES ON SMALL CARDS — IT MEASURES THE ALLOCATOR'S APPETITE, NOT DEMAND. This, not VRAM, is what blocks the cheap GPU tier. Measured across three cards 2026-08-05.

THE DECISIVE TABLE (same recipe, ARM A, micro 1 x accum 32, seq 4096, Liger, post-truncation-fix):
  card             total  alloc  resvd  devwide  dev%   s/it   steps  outcome
  A100 SXM4 80GB   79.25  36.53  60.22    61.87  78.1%  38.06     24  COMPLETED 25
  RTX PRO 5000     47.27  35.89  44.36    44.97  95.1%  36.09      1  ABORTED step 4
  RTX A6000        44.43  35.89  40.49    40.96  92.2%  84.06      3  ABORTED step 4

ALLOCATED PEAK IS 35.9-36.5 GiB ON ALL THREE. Demand is IDENTICAL and fits every card with room. What changes is RESERVED, which expands to fill whatever is present: 76% of the A100, 91% of the A6000, 94% of the PRO 5000. That is normal PyTorch caching-allocator behaviour — it does not return memory it has touched.

THE GUARD FIRES AT 92% OF DEVICE-WIDE `mem_get_info` (`device_pool_gb`/`resolve_mem_limit`, train_orpo_trl.py:81-146). On a small card the allocator's own cache pushes device-wide past 92% as a matter of course, so the guard trips on ITS OWN CACHE. NO OOM WAS EVER OBSERVED on any card. Both cheap-tier aborts are FALSE POSITIVES.

THE TELL WAS IN THE ABORT MESSAGE ALL ALONG: "this process holds nanGB of it". The guard cannot attribute device-wide usage to our process, so it aborts on a total that includes its own reserve. A guard that cannot say what share is ours cannot distinguish "a co-tenant is eating the card" from "our allocator cached a lot".

THE FIX, in order of preference:
 1. Gate on `torch.cuda.memory_allocated()` (real demand, 35.9 GiB) and use device-wide only to detect CO-TENANTS, which was the original purpose. Reserved is not a leak and must not be treated as one.
 2. Or set `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True` so reserve tracks demand.
 3. Do NOT simply raise the 92% — that hides the attribution bug and leaves a guard that still cannot name what it is measuring.
The AMD/GTT branch keeps device-wide semantics: there a co-tenant genuinely shares the pool (see the docstring at :168).

CONSEQUENCE FOR M3 HARDWARE: the cheap tier is probably viable and the VRAM floor of 46 is probably too high — but neither is PROVEN, because no small card has completed 25 steps. Fix the guard, then re-run ONE PRO 5000 probe to settle it. Superseding note 9ea3f5d0's "needs a genuine 48GB card": that conclusion was drawn from guard trips, not OOMs.
