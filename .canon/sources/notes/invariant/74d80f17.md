THE GTT RATCHET IS REAL, IT IS THE TRAINER'S OWN, AND IT CAPS A SINGLE PROCESS AT ~118 STEPS AT SEQ 4096. Proven 2026-08-03 on an EMPTY box with per-process attribution. SUPERSEDES my own note f1e96c88, which concluded from a 150-step accum-1 probe that the trainer was flat and the ratchet was co-tenancy. THAT WAS WRONG — 150 micro-batches was 13x short of the onset, exactly the shortfall f1e96c88 itself flagged and I then under-weighted.

THE MEASUREMENT. Arm A: daemon STOPPED, GTT at launch 620 MiB, nothing else holding a render node. 400 iters requested, micro 1 x accum 32, seq 4096, grad checkpointing.
  steps   1-29 : proc GTT median  4.9-5.1 GB   running max  36.6
  steps  60-68 : proc GTT median 28.4 GB       running max  59.0
  steps 109-118: proc GTT median 46.3 GB       running max 101.3   <- tripwire
torch `memory_allocated` FLAT at 1.73 GB the whole way; `max_memory_allocated` FLAT at 32.6 GB; s/it FLAT (~175 -> 168, no degradation). So: not a tensor leak, not swap thrash — driver/allocator reserve growing monotonically. ~0.6 GB/step of running-max growth means 400 steps needs ~250 GB. NO CONFIGURATION COMPLETES 400 STEPS IN ONE PROCESS, and neither could M0.

WHAT SAVED THE RUN, AND THE GENERAL LESSON. The `--gtt-limit-gb` tripwire (default 95) fired at box GTT 102.0 GB, set should_training_stop, and the run exited 0 having SAVED THE ADAPTER AND RUN THE GATE (max|B| 6.613e-03). M0 hit the same wall as a SIGKILL and lost the adapter, the summary.json, and the desktop session. THE PRE-EXISTING `--rss-limit-gb` TRIPWIRE COULD NEVER HAVE FIRED: at M0's death host RSS was under 1 GB while GTT stood at 100.7. On unified memory, guard GTT, not RSS.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE MIX STUDY: 400 iters/arm must be run as LEGS of <=~100 steps, each a fresh process resuming from the previous adapter. Per-leg seed must differ or the same seed replays the same batches from index 0 (note e0c2dcd7 for MLX; the same hazard exists under TRL). BOTH arms must use the identical leg structure or the comparison stops being matched.

UNTESTED LEAD, now the best one: `PYTORCH_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True` is SILENTLY INERT on ROCm (`c10/hip/HIPAllocatorConfig.h:40` logs "not supported on this platform"). Without expandable segments the caching allocator cannot compact, and micro-batch 1 hands it a DIFFERENT SEQUENCE LENGTH EVERY STEP — a new allocation shape each time, so segments proliferate and reserve grows monotonically. That mechanism predicts this curve exactly. Worth testing: `group_by_length=True` (repeat shapes) or fixed-length padding (one shape) — the latter costs ~2.2x compute at seq 4096 vs a p50 of 1,825 tokens.
