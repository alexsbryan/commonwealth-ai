# THE "GTT RATCHET" IS SEQUENCE-SHAPE CHURN IN TORCH'S ALLOCATOR RESERVE, NOT A LEAK. AT A SINGLE FIXED SHAPE THE TRAINER REACHES STEADY…

THE "GTT RATCHET" IS SEQUENCE-SHAPE CHURN IN TORCH'S ALLOCATOR RESERVE, NOT A LEAK. AT A SINGLE FIXED SHAPE THE TRAINER REACHES STEADY STATE AND NEVER GROWS AGAIN. Supersedes note 74d80f17, which called it a monotonic ~0.6 GB/step ratchet needing ~250 GB for 400 steps and concluded that NO config completes an arm in one process. That conclusion is wrong.

Measured 2026-08-03 on the Halo, empty box, scripts/shape_ratchet_probe.py. Real Qwen3.5-0.8B, real LoRA r32/a64 on the same 186 modules, bf16, gradient checkpointing, forward+backward+AdamW. 60 iterations per arm, ONE variable: the sequence length fed each iteration.

  arm     shapes  reserved             alloc_peak  proc_gtt max   tokens   wall
  vary        37  3.48 -> 82.88 GB     28.661      82.88        319,744    521s
  fixed        1  37.91 then FLAT      28.661      38.56        491,520   1002s

THE CONTROL IS THE RESULT. The fixed arm rises to 37.91 GB in the first few iterations and then reads 37.91 for all 60 -- zero drops >1 GB, 59/59 iterations rose-or-held, min-after-iter-30 = 37.91. That is an allocator reaching its working set and stopping. The vary arm oscillates over a 24x range with 18 drops >1 GB. alloc_peak is IDENTICAL (28.661 GB) in both, so live-memory demand is the same; only the RESERVE differs. The fixed arm did MORE work (1.54x tokens, 1.92x wall) and reserved LESS THAN HALF. Compute does not explain it; shape variety does.

WHY EVERY EARLIER READING WAS WRONG. proc_gtt_gb reads drm-resident-gtt, sampled ONCE per optimizer step. Three facts kill the leak story and all three were already in runs/mix-A/steps.jsonl before any new run:
  * gpu_peak_gb (max_memory_allocated) is PINNED at 32.56 GB from step 2 to step 118. Torch's peak demand never grows.
  * The proc-GTT FLOOR never rises. Step 113 reads 5.7 GB after ~3,616 micro-batches; step 118 reads 101.3. A leak raises the floor.
  * In runs/ab-baseline, torch's peak stops growing at step 62 (23.88 GB) yet every GTT spike lands at step 93+ and reaches 42.6 GB -- 1.8x torch's all-time peak, appearing without torch allocating anything new.
What actually rose over a run was the FREQUENCY of high samples in a bimodal variable. The "0.6 GB/step -> 250 GB" figure is a rising MEDIAN of that variable, not a leak rate.

GTT IS TORCH'S RESERVE, FULL STOP. Across all 120 probe iterations, proc_gtt - reserved is a CONSTANT <=1.24 GB with exactly ONE drm client. Nothing grows outside torch: no HIP-runtime creep, no Triton kernel-cache accumulation. num_alloc_retries is 0 and segment count is flat (277-306) in BOTH arms, so this is also not classic fragmentation or allocator thrash -- it is reserve sized to whatever shape just arrived.

WHAT KILLED ARM A. train_orpo_trl.py:506 trips on a SINGLE INSTANTANEOUS SAMPLE (`if box == box and box > args.gtt_limit_gb`) with no sustained-level or rolling-median requirement, and launch_arm.sh sets --gtt-limit-gb 95. The shape-churn transient runs to ~2.9x alloc_peak (82.88/28.661 measured here; 101.3/32.56 = 3.11x in arm A), i.e. a ceiling of ~95-100 GB for this workload. THE LIMIT WAS SET BELOW THE WORKLOAD'S NORMAL TRANSIENT, so it was certain to fire eventually; step 118 is merely when the once-per-step sampler happened to catch one. Nothing was accumulating and nothing was near OOM.

THE FIX, AND WHAT IT VOIDS. Bucket or pad sequence lengths so shapes repeat (group_by_length=True, or pad up to a small set of length buckets). Predicted steady reserve for arm A: ~1.32x alloc_peak = ~43 GB against 125 GB of unified memory. SINGLE-PROCESS 400 STEPS IS THEREFORE POSSIBLE. This voids the plan of record in frame 382c4d04: the <=100-step leg structure, the per-leg seed rotation, and the launch_arm.sh --resume wrapper are all unnecessary and should not be built. Also fix the tripwire to require N consecutive samples over the limit, and raise the limit above ~3x alloc_peak.

COST TRADE. Full fixed-length padding at 4096 was 1.92x wall clock here, matching the ~2.2x predicted from p50 1,825. group_by_length is the cheaper middle path and should be tried first: at micro-batch 1 it orders samples by length so consecutive steps present near-identical shapes and segments get reused, without paying max-length padding on every example.
