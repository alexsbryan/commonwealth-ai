# A PEER SESSION'S BROAD git add WILL SWEEP YOUR UNCOMMITTED WORK INTO ITS COMMIT. Twice on 2026-08-20 in this shared checkout, and both…

A PEER SESSION'S BROAD `git add` WILL SWEEP YOUR UNCOMMITTED WORK INTO ITS COMMIT. Twice on 2026-08-20 in this shared checkout, and both times the message described something else entirely.

OCCURRENCE 1: `ba3ffcbd` — the nc-2c-A worker's `resolve_self_node_id` change, in its own words "my code, committed by a peer sweeping my working tree".
OCCURRENCE 2: `ac8f65a0` "docs(convergence): the unnamed half" — a census doc commit that also carries `scripts/nc-thesis.py` (+125) and 28 lines of `quality/campaigns/noun-convergence.toml`, both authored by the comaintainer seat minutes earlier. The seat's own follow-up commit was left holding 5 lines while its drafted message described 153 lines it no longer contained.

WHY IT HAPPENS: several sessions (seat + workers + peer workstreams) share ONE working tree on this host. `git add -A`, `git add .`, and `git commit -a` are all tree-wide, not session-wide. Nothing in git scopes a stage to a session, so whoever commits first takes everything uncommitted, including files another session is mid-edit on.

WHAT TO DO:
1. Stage by explicit path, always. `git add path/one path/two`, never `-A`, never `.`, never `commit -a`. This does not protect you from a peer, but it stops you being the peer.
2. **Read `git show --stat HEAD` after every commit.** If the file count or insertion count does not match what you wrote, something else came along or something of yours left. This is the only cheap detector; the commit succeeds either way and says nothing.
3. If your message overclaims because content went elsewhere, amend YOUR OWN commit — do not rewrite the peer's. Cross-reference where the content actually landed, by sha, in the body. A peer's commit may already have work on top of it, and rewriting it breaks their clone (see the standing rule against `scripts/strip-coauthors.sh`).
4. Commit in small windows. The exposure is the interval between edit and commit. Two commits 90 seconds apart is what let occurrence 2 through.

WHAT NOT TO DO: do not "fix" this by having sessions coordinate through a lock file or by serializing commits. The checkout is shared by design and the fan-out is the point. Detection plus honest cross-referencing is the cheap correct response; the alternative costs more than the defect.
