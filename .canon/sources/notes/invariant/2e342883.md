TRL AND MLX TRUNCATE OPPOSITE ENDS OF AN ORPO ROW. For a grounding verifier that is the difference between a harmless cut and a destroyed label.

- TRL (`ORPOConfig.max_prompt_length`) truncates the PROMPT. In verifier-v0 the prompt IS the evidence document, so this cuts the evidence and leaves a label the model cannot verify.
- mlx-lm-lora has NO `max_prompt_length` (grep the package: zero matches). `iterate_orpo_batches` (trainer/orpo_trainer.py:123) tail-truncates at `max_seq_length`, cutting the COMPLETION.

MEASURED 2026-08-02, Qwen3.5 tokenizer, 6,000-row seeded samples (`research/verifier-v0/scripts/measure_truncation.py`):
- Stream A (orpo-76k): prompt p50 835 / p99 1710 / max 2761; >2048 prompt 0.28%; >4096 seq 0.02%
- Stream B (stream_b pairs): prompt p50 823 / p99 2481 / max 2637; >2048 prompt 1.65%; >4096 seq 0.00%

CARRY THIS: Stream B runs 6x hotter than Stream A against a 2048 prompt cap. Inert on the MLX lane (nothing meaningful hits 4096). LIVE the moment M3 runs on TRL — the Halo lane or a rented GPU — where it would cut evidence out of B rows at six times A's rate and silently bias the M2 mix study against B. If M3 leaves MLX, set `max_prompt_length` >= 2688 or drop the cap.

ALSO CHECKED, not a problem: `iterate_orpo_batches` sorts the whole dataset by completion length (:97) so micro-batches are length-homogeneous, and B's rows are systematically shorter (p50 985 vs 1816) — i.e. B concentrates into its own micro-batches. Safe anyway because batch ORDER is re-permuted each epoch (:116) and one optimizer step averages 8 micro-batches under --gradient-accumulation-steps 8. This length-bucketing is also the likely reason the Halo's "bigger micro-batch was slower" padding finding does not reproduce on MLX.
