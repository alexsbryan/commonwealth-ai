So a peer's `samples` never leaves 0, which pins `cold_start_weight` at COLD_START_MIN_WEIGHT (0.7) forever. That LOOKS like the obvious other half of F9 to fix.

Measured: freezing peer samples is worth −11% to −33% mean latency on three of five fleets (pair, household-evening-12, heterogeneous-fleet), against a +3% cost on twin-hubs. Wiring it up is a REGRESSION.

The mechanism is monotone, three points on one axis (heterogeneous-fleet p50, reference seed):
  warm-start (peers always 1.0) ........ 120.7s
  as-implemented (peers ramp) ..........  33.7s
  blind-peer-ramp (peers always 0.7) ...  16.9s
The more attractive a peer looks, the more this fleet over-offloads and the worse it does. On a fleet where the peer is not DECISIVELY better, anything that raises peer scores uniformly buys queueing, not throughput.

ONE HONEST LIMIT, and the first experiment to run if the brake is ever replaced: `throughput_factor` gates its source-of-truth on `samples >= THROUGHPUT_OBSERVATION_THRESHOLD` (scoring.rs:231), so a frozen `samples` ALSO means the mesh measures peer throughput on every stream (`ThroughputTarget::Peer` keeps `tg_tok_s_ewma` current) and then declines to read it. The 0.7 brake and the discarded throughput signal are ENTANGLED in `blind-peer-ramp` and were not separated. A peer scored on its observed rate is a different policy from a peer scored at a flat 0.7, and only one of them is defensible.
