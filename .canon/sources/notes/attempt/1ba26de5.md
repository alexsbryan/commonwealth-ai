# Three embed-perf fixes that made things worse or no difference, in order.

Three embed-perf fixes that made things worse or no difference, in order.

1. **Bigger packed batches (`n_ctx=8192, n_seq_max=64`)** — tripped `NoKvCacheSlot` at decode time. llama.cpp's KV scheduler partitions the unified cache: per-slot budget ≈ `n_ctx / n_seq_max` tokens. With 64 slots and 8192 ctx, each slot got 128 tokens. Wikipedia chunks are 200-1500 tokens. First long chunk crashed.
   → Fix direction: `n_ctx / n_seq_max >= max_input_tokens`. Current: `n_ctx=16384, n_seq_max=16, max_input_tokens=1024`.

2. True multi-sequence batched decode — correct on its own, modest win (~15%), far short of needed. Naming was a lie before: `run_embed_batch_sync` was a sequential loop despite its doc comment claiming parallel GPU decode. Fixing the naming was necessary but not sufficient.
   → Lesson: matched names to behaviour, good hygiene, but don't expect throughput from a bin-packer alone when the per-sub-batch compute is CPU-bound.

3. **`n_threads=available_parallelism()`** — actively regressed 15%. llama-cpp-2 default 4 was wrong; 12 on an 8P+4E chip is wronger. Peaks at P-core count (see invariant note on `llama_threads_for_host`).
   → Lesson: verify scaling with a bench before picking a constant. The default was 4 for a reason (worst-case-safe); our "fix" assumed scaling continues past P-cores.

The actual fix was Metal offload (12× speedup). Took benchmarking to realize the CPU ceiling was ~2.8 seq/sec regardless of knob tuning, and that Metal's old crash was long-since fixed.
