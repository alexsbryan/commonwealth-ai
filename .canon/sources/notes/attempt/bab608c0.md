DAEMON RSS: THE ARENA MECHANISM IS UNCONFIRMED. FOUR ATTEMPTS, NO REPRODUCTION. Read this before repeating the investigation — it says what is solid, what I over-claimed, and what would actually settle it.

WHAT IS SOLID (static, repeatable, measured 2026-09-12 on RuggedFox, pid 2008470, 20h uptime, 32 cores)
- The memory is ANONYMOUS, not GPU and not model mmap. /proc/<pid>/smaps aggregated by backing: 18.80 GB [anon], 1.96 GB [heap], 0.09 GB binary; Anonymous 21.78 of 21.88 GB RSS. The /dev/dri/renderD128 mappings and the GGUF are large VIRTUAL and ~zero RESIDENT. NEVER read sizes out of /proc/<pid>/maps and conclude anything — they are virtual, and doing so is what makes people blame unified memory on Strix Halo.
- ~85 anonymous mappings are exactly 64 MiB or 128 MiB AND 64 MiB-aligned — the glibc `HEAP_MAX_SIZE` heap signature (the 128 MiB ones are adjacent pairs coalesced by /proc/maps display). That is ~127 heaps, about 8 GB.
- No `#[global_allocator]` anywhere in the workspace (no jemalloc/mimalloc/snmalloc). `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX` unset in the daemon env. `max_blocking_threads` appears NOWHERE in the workspace, so every tokio runtime here runs the default cap of 512. 61 `spawn_blocking` sites are reachable from the daemon.
- The daemon runtime is built at `sovereign/crates/sovereign-cli-daemon/src/lib.rs:215` with `.thread_stack_size(8MB)` and `.thread_name(...)` and no blocking-pool cap.

WHAT I OVER-CLAIMED AND AM RETRACTING
- I said ~15 GB was arenas, inferred from a resident-size histogram. DIRECT counting gives ~8 GB. The other ~10 GB of anon is unattributed.
- I asserted a causal chain — blocking-pool bursts create arenas that outlive their threads — as though established. It is not. It is a plausible reading of a static fingerprint and I failed to demonstrate it four times.

THE FOUR FAILED CONFIRMATIONS, so nobody repeats them
1. 12 concurrent `sovereign-cli tools call code_search`: threads flat 46, RSS -41 MB. INVALID — I sent output to /dev/null and never proved the work reached the daemon; the CLI may run ToolRegistry in-process.
2. 16 concurrent `corpus_search` over HTTP /mcp: 6 of 16 responses, all `Invalid input: corpus_search requires 'embedding'` — no work done. Sampler also died at 47 samples, killed by my own `wait`. INVALID twice over.
3. 12 concurrent /v1/chat/completions, sampler fixed with setsid (237 samples, 23.7 s): only 1 of 12 returned. The daemon SERIALIZES inference on its fast slot, so the concurrency was in my curl clients and never in the daemon's blocking pool. Threads 49->50, arenas 85->85, RSS +65 MB. Not a test of the hypothesis.
4. 200 s idle watch, sampled every 20 s: threads flat 49, arenas flat 85, RSS oscillating ~80 MB and ENDING 75 MB LOWER than it started. So the daemon is NOT leaking at idle either — it is sitting at a plateau it reached earlier in its life.

WHAT THE FAILURES ACTUALLY TEACH
The daemon is at a stable high-water plateau, not in active growth. Arena count did not move under anything I could drive from outside. So the 22 GB was established during real work earlier in its 20 h — most plausibly an ingest or enrichment run — and black-box poking at a quiet daemon cannot reproduce it.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT, in order
1. Sample `ls /proc/<pid>/task | wc -l` and the arena count THROUGH A REAL INGEST OR ENRICHMENT RUN, which is when the blocking pool would actually burst. Cheap, non-invasive, and it is the observation all four attempts above failed to make.
2. The A/B: restart with `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2`, run a COMPARABLE workload, compare the plateau. This is the only thing that proves or disproves the arena story, and it needs the same workload both sides or it proves nothing (ARCH §7).
3. Only then consider `.max_blocking_threads(N)` or a `#[global_allocator]`. Do not ship a fix for a mechanism nobody has watched happen (ARCH §5).
