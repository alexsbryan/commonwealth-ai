# Deep research — demo plan (product practice)

Operator direction 2026-08-14: engineering has to prove the value to product
now. Every chunk in the initiative closes with a demoable outcome for the
product team — an artifact a non-engineer can read and be convinced by, not a
bench table. The demos map to the spine: each one makes one spine mechanism
visible in the product.

The acceptance of every demo is the OPERATOR HOLDOUT: the operator runs a
question of their own choosing in the product themselves; their read of the
answer is terminal authority — no benchmark overrides it (the
E-operator-holdout pattern, applied per chunk).

## DEMO-1 — "the loop thinks" (after T1a: the thin local-only loop)

**The scene.** One research question, asked in the product. The system
surveys what it already owns (honestly: nothing yet), attempts an answer,
names its gaps, goes and finds the evidence, and returns a report where every
claim carries a verdict — supported with a chunk citation, flagged, or
honestly open. Then the same question is asked AGAIN: the estate already has
the answer, so the run costs seconds and fetches nothing.

**What the product team sees:**
1. The journey — the gaps driving acquisition, visible in the stage strip,
   not vibes.
2. The artifact — the verdict-stamped report: supported claims with
   citations, flagged claims named, an explicit Open questions section, and
   the manifest (what was fetched, what failed, what was spent, what was NOT
   covered).
3. The compounding — the re-ask: near-zero new fetches, the wall-time
   collapse stated. The estate is the product moat; this is it, made visible.

**Proof carried:** the loop works and is honest. An artifact a product lead
can hold. (Engineering measures later in T1b whether the loop is also
BETTER — DEMO-2.)

## DEMO-2 — "the loop proves it" (after T1b: the measurement arms)

**The scene.** The same product, now with receipts: the coverage floor
(58/72) cleared by the local-only arm; the poisoned-source run where a
confident fabrication planted in the fetched evidence is gated out of the
report and a prompt-injection steered nothing — shown as a before/after
run pair; and the two-arm comparison: the loop vs a one-shot RAG answer on
the same questions, with the loop's lift stated.

**Proof carried:** the loop BUYS something — measured, not asserted. The
single question every skeptic asks ("why not just ask the model?") gets its
answer from the product's own numbers.

## DEMO-3 — "the frontier, on our terms" (after T2: egress + the one frontier role)

**The scene.** The same product with a BYO frontier key. The synthesist runs
on the frontier model — but only ever sees the distilled window of public-web
evidence, through the custody boundary. A personal corpus is structurally
unreachable by the remote slot, demonstrated live: the payload that would
include it is refused by name. The cost and honesty numbers land beside the
named proxy arm.

**Proof carried:** frontier quality at electricity prices with the stance
intact — the initiative's thesis, as a product experience rather than a
claim.

## What the demos are NOT

- Not a bench lane or a debug view. The product path, shown as the operator
  experiences it.
- Not synthetic happy paths: the poisoned drill (DEMO-2) is the honesty
  demo, and it stays in the plan deliberately.
- Not a substitute for the bars: bars measure; demos convince. Both land in
  the same commits.

## Cadence

One demo per chunk, scheduled with the chunk's landing review, accepted by
the operator holdout. A chunk that lands bars but cannot demo fails its
product obligation — the demo is part of done-when, by this plan.
