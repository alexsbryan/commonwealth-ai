Judge Amit Mehta (US District Court for the District of Columbia) ruled on 2024-08-05 that Google is a monopolist in general search services and general search text advertising, violating Section 2 of the Sherman Act. The theory of harm was default-distribution deals: Google paying Apple, Samsung, Mozilla and others billions to be the default search engine — Google's CEO Sundar Pichai testified the company paid about $26.3 billion for distribution in 2021, and trial evidence showed Google paid Apple roughly 36% of Safari search-ad revenue. On 2025-08-05, Judge Mehta ordered structural remedies: Google must sell Chrome (the first forced divestiture of a major tech company since the Microsoft case of 2000), may no longer pay for default search placement (no revenue-sharing agreements), and faces limits on acquisitions and AI-related investments. The ruling established that paying for default placement in search distribution is exclusionary conduct — defaults create scale, scale creates data, data creates quality, quality protects the default, a self-reinforcing loop rivals could not break. Google appealed to the DC Circuit (notice filed 2025), arguing the trial court punished procompetitive innovation; the case's final outcome remained pending through 2025.
