The global IT outage in July 2024 was caused by a faulty CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor content update released on **July 19, 2024** [Source: ev-1]. The root cause was identified as an out-of-bounds memory read in the sensor's C++ code, specifically an untrusted pointer dereference triggered by a **21**-byte input that bypassed flawed validation checks [Source: ev-1]. This incident highlighted the risks of monoculture concentration, where a single security agent deployed at enterprise scale became a global single point of failure due to fast-cadence updates [Source: ev-1].

The consequences were severe and widespread. Approximately **8.5** million Windows devices crashed into blue-screen loops, according to Microsoft’s estimate [Source: ev-1]. Key sectors including airlines, banks, hospitals, broadcasters, and **911** services experienced significant disruptions [Source: ev-1]. Delta Air Lines cancelled roughly **7,000** flights and incurred approximately $**500** million in costs, leading them to sue CrowdStrike and hire David Boies [Source: ev-1]. Financially, CrowdStrike shares fell about **11%** on the following Monday, July 22, 2024 [Source: ev-1]. In response, CEO George Kurtz testified before Congress on September 24, 2024, while the company apologized and implemented changes such as staged rollouts with canary testing and added sensor-level validation [Source: ev-1].
