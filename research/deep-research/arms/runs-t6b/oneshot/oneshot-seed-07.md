The United States moved to force TikTok's sale or ban it primarily due to national security concerns, specifically the potential for Chinese government access to US user data and the app’s algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review and the "Project Texas" initiative, which aimed to host US user data in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legally, President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on 2024-04-24, requiring ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a ban [Source: ev-1]. The Supreme Court upheld this law with a unanimous 9-0 decision on 2025-01-17 [Source: ev-1). Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US on 2025/01/18/19 for approximately 14 hours before being restored on 2025.01.19 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement [Source: ev-1]. On 2025.01.20, an executive order delayed enforcement for another 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was ultimately resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on 2025.06.10 [Source: ev-1]. This agreement structured the resolution as a joint venture rather than an outright sale, with Vice President JD Vance leading White House negotiations [Source: ev-1]. Under this structure, Oracle received about a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations, while SoftBank and other investors participated, and ByteDance retained a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. The final ownership structure was completed by 2025 [Source: ev-1].
