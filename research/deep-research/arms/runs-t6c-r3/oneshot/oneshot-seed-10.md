OpenAI released GPT-5 on August 7, 2025 [Source: ev-1] to consolidate its product strategy around a single unified model rather than maintaining separate reasoning and non-reasoning products. This release marked a deliberate shift in how the company sells its models by adopting "give-away-the-frontier" pricing strategies that prioritize aggressive free-tier distribution over high-cost access barriers [Source: ev-1].

Key changes to OpenAI's sales approach include:
*   **Pricing Structure:** The model is available for free on the base tier, while paid tiers are set at $20/month for Plus (unlimited GPT-5), $200/month for Pro, and API rates of $1.25 per million input tokens / $10 per million output tokens for GPT-5, with lower costs ($0.25/$2) for GPT-5 mini [Source: ev-1]. This represented a significant price collapse compared to previous o-series models [Source: ev-1].
*   **Product Consolidation:** OpenAI retired separate products like o3, o4-mini, o3-mini, and o3-pro, folding their capabilities into internal "reasoning modes" within GPT-5 [Source: ev-1]. The company eliminated the need for users to pick between different model types by positioning GPT-5 as one model that simply "thinks," rather than distinguishing it specifically as a reasoning model [Source: ev-1].
*   **Competitive Focus:** By offering features such as a 5-million-token context window, native image generation, deep research integration, file upload, and web search out of the box at these lower price points (with GPT-5 scoring approximately 78.6% on SWE-bench Verified), OpenAI moved the competitive battleground from benchmark demos to becoming the default agency—focusing on models that act rather than just answer [Source: ev-1].
