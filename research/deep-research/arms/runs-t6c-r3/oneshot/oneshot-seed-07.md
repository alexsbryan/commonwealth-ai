The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok between 2024 and 2025 primarily due to national security concerns, specifically regarding potential Chinese government access to US user data and the platform's algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review and efforts such as "Project Texas," which aimed to host US user data in Oracle’s cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legally, President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on April 24, 2024, requiring ByteDance to divest its US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by an additional 90 days) or face a ban in the United States [Source: ev-1]. The Supreme Court upheld this law with a unanimous 9-0 decision on January 17, 2025, just one day before the effective date of January 19, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US for approximately 14 hours around January 18/19, 2023, but was restored on January 19 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement via an executive order issued on January 20, which delayed enforcement by 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was ultimately resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on June 10, 2025. Under this agreement, Oracle acquired a stake of about 12.5% in TikTok's US operations, with participation from SoftBank and other investors [Source: ev-1]. The resolution structured the entity as a joint venture rather than an outright sale, allowing ByteDance to retain a majority interest while ensuring that Vice President JD Vance’s White House negotiations addressed national security requirements [Source: ev-1].
