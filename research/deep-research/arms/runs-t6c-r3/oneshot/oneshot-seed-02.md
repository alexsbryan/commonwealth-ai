DeepSeek’s R1 release triggered the largest single-day loss in Nvidia's history because it demonstrated that a frontier-class, open-weights reasoning model could be trained at a fraction of the cost previously assumed necessary, thereby challenging the market thesis that demand for Nvidia’s chips was secured by high barriers to entry based on compute scale [Source: ev-1]. Specifically, DeepSeek released R1 on January 20, 2025 [Source: ev-1], following its predecessor DeepSeek-V3 (released December 26, 2024), which reported training costs of approximately US$5.6 million using roughly 2.79 million GPU-hours on export-restricted H800 chips [Source: ev-1].

This revelation exposed that efficiency driven by scarcity—specifically due to U.S. export controls restricting access to advanced hardware like the H800—had forced Chinese labs to optimize their economics rather than relying solely on massive capital expenditure for compute scale [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, markets repriced this shift in frontier-AI training economics, leading Nvidia to lose approximately US$589 billion in market capitalization on January 27, 2025, which was recorded as the largest single-day loss in stock-market history at the time and resulted in about US$1 trillion being erased across the tech complex [Source: ev-1].
