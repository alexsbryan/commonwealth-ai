NASA ordered Boeing's Starliner to return uncrewed from its first crewed flight because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during the docking approach, and NASA determined that these anomalies could not be guaranteed safe for the deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. The decision was made on 2024-08-24 after it became clear that the affected thrusters could not be ground-tested in their specific flight condition [Source: ev-1].

This decision had significant operational and financial implications for the program. Operationally, astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams were stranded aboard the International Space Station; they returned to Earth on a SpaceX Crew-9 Dragon capsule on 2025-03-18, spending roughly 9.5 months aloft compared to the planned ~8 days [Source: ev-1]. Financially and strategically, the incident highlighted ongoing struggles under Boeing's fixed-price NASA contract from the 2014 Commercial Crew program (where Boeing was allocated about $4.2B versus SpaceX’s about $2.6B) [Source: ev-1]. By 2025, Boeing had disclosed cumulative program losses of more than $1.5B [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, in June 2025, Boeing announced approximately 400 job cuts in its space division, and as of 2025, NASA's path to certifying Starliner for regular crewed operations remained unresolved [Source: ev-1].
