OpenAI released o3 and o4-mini on April 16, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. The primary strategic reason for this launch was to defend the frontier of reasoning models against competitive pressures from DeepSeek’s R1 cost shock and Anthropic’s push into agentic coding [Source: ev-1]. By introducing these models before GPT-5 shipped, OpenAI aimed to reframe frontier capability as tool-using reasoning available at commodity prices [Source: ev-1].

The launch signaled a specific direction for future frontier reasoning models characterized by three key shifts:
1. **Native Integration:** It marked the first time OpenAI built vision directly into its reasoning models rather than bolting it on, while also training native tool use (such as web search and code interpreter) into the model itself [Source: ev-1].
2. **Dynamic Compute via Budget Forcing:** The technique employed was "budget forcing," which utilizes a fixed maximum thought budget with task-adaptive dynamic compute, allowing harder questions to trigger longer thinking processes [Source: ev-1].
3. **Privacy of Reasoning Processes:** It introduced OpenAI’s first private chain-of-thought policy, ensuring that reasoning tokens are not exposed to API callers [Source: ev-1].

Additionally, the launch demonstrated significant performance gains; o3 set a record on the ARC-AGI benchmark by achieving approximately 87.5% on ARC-AGI-1 at high compute levels, meeting or exceeding the ~85% human baseline and becoming the first model to reach this threshold [Source: ev-1]. The release was also notable for its API-first approach, making both models available to developers immediately (with o4-mini in the ChatGPT app), while pricing them competitively at $10/$40 per million input/output tokens for o3 and $1.10/$4.40 for o4-mini [Source: ev-1].
