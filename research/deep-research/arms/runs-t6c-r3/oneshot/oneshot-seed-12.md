Microsoft's Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because it was initially designed to take screenshots of the user's screen every few seconds and store them locally, creating a searchable "photographic memory" [Source: ev-1]. Despite Microsoft’s claims that this data would be encrypted, security researchers demonstrated that the screenshots were actually stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. This storage method meant that sensitive content visible on the screen, including passwords and private messages, could be accessed by any process running as the user [Source: ev-1]. The collision between the feature's default-on recording capability and standard privacy expectations led regulatory bodies such as the Irish Data Protection Commission, the UK Information Commissioner's Office, and the Washington State Attorney General to raise questions or open investigations [Source: ev-1].

In response to these concerns, Microsoft delayed the release in June 2024 and subsequently reversed its default settings [Source: ev-1]. The revised version of Recall is now opt-in (default OFF) rather than enabled by default [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, accessing the timeline requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection, while incognito mode and DRM content are explicitly excluded from capture [Source: ev-1]. This updated, privacy-focused version reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
