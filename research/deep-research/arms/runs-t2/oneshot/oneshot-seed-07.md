The United States moved to force TikTok’s sale or ban it primarily due to national security concerns regarding potential Chinese government access to US user data and the platform's algorithm, which had been subject to CFIUS review [Source: ev-1]. To address these risks, President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on 2024-04-24, which mandated that ByteDance divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a ban in the US [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on 2025-06-10. Under this agreement, Oracle acquired approximately a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations, with participation from SoftBank and other investors, while ByteDance retained a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. The resolution structured the entity as a joint venture rather than an outright sale, implementing "Project Texas" to ensure US user data remained in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1]. This process involved significant legal and executive actions; notably, after the Supreme Court upheld the law 9-0 on 2025-17 (one day before its effective date of 2025-20), President Trump issued an executive order on 2025 that delayed enforcement for 75 days following TikTok’s brief blackout [Source: ev-1].
