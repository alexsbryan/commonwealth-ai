NASA ordered Boeing’s Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA determined that these thrusters could not be guaranteed safe for the deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. The decision was made explicit on 2024-08-24 after the craft had docked with the station on 2024/06/06 following a launch on 2024–06–05 [Source: ev-1]. A key factor in this safety determination was that the anomalous thrusters could not be ground-tested under their specific flight conditions to verify reliability before re-entry [Source: ev-1].

For the program, this decision resulted in astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams remaining aboard the International Space Station until they returned via a Crew-9 Dragon spacecraft on 2025/03/18, extending their mission from a planned ~8 days to roughly 9.5 months aloft [Source: ev-1]. The uncrewed return itself occurred after Starliner undocked on 2024‑09‑06 and landed at White Sands on 2024−09−07 [Source: ev-1]. Beyond the immediate flight impact, the incident highlighted significant financial and operational challenges for Boeing; under its fixed-price contract from the 2014 Commercial Crew program (valued at approximately $4.2B compared to SpaceX’s $2.6B), Boeing disclosed cumulative program losses of more than $1.5B by 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, in June 2025, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division, while NASA's path toward certifying Starliner remained unresolved throughout 2025 [Source: ev-1].
