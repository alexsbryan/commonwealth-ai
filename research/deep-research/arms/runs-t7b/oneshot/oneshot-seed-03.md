NASA ordered Boeing’s Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA could not guarantee that these thrusters would be safe for the critical deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. This decision was made on August 24, 2024, after it became clear that the affected thrusters could not be ground-tested in their flight condition to verify safety [Source: ev-1].

The decision had several significant implications for the program:
*   **Extended Crew Stay:** Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams were stranded on the International Space Station until March 18, 2025. They returned via a SpaceX Dragon capsule (Crew-9) after spending roughly 9.5 months aloft, compared to the originally planned duration of about 8 days [Source: ev-1].
*   **Financial Losses:** The issues contributed to Boeing disclosing cumulative program losses of more than $1.5 billion by 2025, despite operating under a fixed-price contract from the 2014 Commercial Crew program valued at approximately $4.2 billion for Boeing (compared to SpaceX’s ~$2.6 billion) [Source: ev-1].
*   **Workforce Reductions and Certification Delays:** In June 2025, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division, and NASA's path toward fully certifying Starliner remained unresolved through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
