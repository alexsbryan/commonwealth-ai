The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok between 2024 and 2025 primarily due to national security concerns, specifically the potential for Chinese government access to US user data and the platform's algorithm [Source: ev-1]. These concerns were formalized through CFIUS review and the implementation of "Project Texas," which involved hosting US user data in Oracle’s cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legally, President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on April 24, 2024, requiring ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a ban [Source: ev-1]. The Supreme Court upheld this law with a 9-0 per curiam decision on January 17, 2025, one day before its effective date of January 19, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US for approximately 14 hours between January 18/19 and was restored on January 19, 2 dispute resolution involved President Trump pledging to pause enforcement; his executive order dated January 20, 2025 delayed enforcement for an additional 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was ultimately resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on June 10, 2025 per Bloomberg reporting [Source: ev-1]. This agreement structured the outcome as a joint venture rather than an outright sale, granting Oracle about a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations with participation from SoftBank and other investors [Source: ev-1]. While Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations, ByteDance retained a majority interest in the company [Source: ev-1]. The final ownership structure under this arrangement was completed during 2025 [Source: ev-1].
