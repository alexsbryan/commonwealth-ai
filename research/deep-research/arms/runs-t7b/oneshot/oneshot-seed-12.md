Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 primarily because security researchers discovered that the screenshots it captured were stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database, contradicting Microsoft's claims of encryption [Source: ev-1]. This vulnerability meant that sensitive content visible on screen, such as passwords and private messages, was accessible to any process running under the user account by default [Source: ev-1]. The feature had been announced on May 20, 2024, at the Copilot+ PC launch with this "photographic memory" capability enabled by default, leading to significant backlash from regulators like the Irish Data Protection Commission and the UK Information Commissioner’s Office, as well as an investigation opened by Washington State Attorney General Bob Ferguson [Source: ev-1].

In response to these security concerns and privacy expectations regarding local data, Microsoft delayed the release in June 2024 and fundamentally changed how Recall operates [Source: ev-1]. The revised version is now opt-in (default OFF) rather than automatically active [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, accessing the timeline now requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection, and the feature explicitly excludes incognito browsing sessions and DRM content [Source: ev-1]. This updated, more secure version reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
