Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because, despite Microsoft's claims that data was encrypted, security researchers demonstrated that the screenshots were stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. This storage method made sensitive information captured by default—including passwords and other private content visible on screen—accessible to any process running as the user [Source: ev-1]. The controversy escalated with inquiries from regulatory bodies such as the Irish Data Protection Commission and the UK Information Commissioner's Office, alongside an investigation opened by Washington State Attorney General Bob Ferguson [Source: ev-1].

In response to these security failures and privacy concerns, Microsoft initially delayed the feature in June 2024 before reversing its design approach [Source: ev-1]. The relaunched version of Recall is now opt-in (default OFF) rather than enabled automatically [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, accessing the timeline requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection, while incognito and DRM content are explicitly excluded from capture [Source: ev-1]. This revised, more secure version reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
