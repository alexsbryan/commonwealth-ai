NASA ordered Boeing's Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach on 2024-06-06, and these anomalies could not be guaranteed safe for the deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. The decision was made because the thrusters could not be ground-tested in their flight condition, making it impossible to verify their safety for re-entry [Source: ev-1].

This decision had significant implications for the program. Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams were stranded aboard the International Space Station from when they launched on 2024-uncrewed return announcement date of 2024-undocked on 2025-03-18 via a Crew-9 Dragon capsule, spending roughly 9.5 months aloft instead of the planned ~8 days [Source: ev-1]. Financially, Boeing disclosed cumulative program losses of more than $1.5B by 2025 under its fixed-price NASA contract (which was approximately $4.2B compared to SpaceX's $2.6B) originating from the 2014 Commercial Crew program [Source: ev-1]. Operationally, in June 2025, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division, and NASA's certification path for Starliner remained unresolved through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
