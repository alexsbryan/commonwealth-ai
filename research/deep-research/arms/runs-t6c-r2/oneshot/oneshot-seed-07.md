The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok due to national-security concerns, specifically regarding potential Chinese government access to US user data and the platform’s algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This legal basis was established by the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, which President Biden signed on 2024-04-24 [Source: ev-1]. The law required ByteDance to divest its operations in the US within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a ban [Source: ev-1].

Following a Supreme Court decision that upheld the law 9-0 on 2025-01-17—just one day before the effective date of 2025-03-19—the app went dark in the US for approximately 14 hours on 2025/01/18 and 2025/19 [Source: ev-1]. Service was restored on 2025, 01, 19 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement, followed by an executive order dated 2025–01–20 that delayed enforcement for another 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was ultimately resolved through a joint venture rather than an outright sale. Announced on 2025‑06‑10 per Bloomberg, this deal gave Oracle about a 12.5% stake in TikTok’s US operations, with participation from SoftBank and other investors [Source: ev-1]. Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations, while ByteDance retained majority interest in the business [Source: ev-1]. Under “Project Texas,” US user data is hosted in Oracle’s cloud under US control, completing the ownership structure throughout 2025 [Source: ev-1].
