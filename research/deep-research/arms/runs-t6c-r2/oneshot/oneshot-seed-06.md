On July 19, 2024 [Source: ev-1], a faulty CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor content update caused a global IT outage by crashing Windows machines into blue-screen loops due to an out-of-bounds memory read in the sensor's C++ code [Source: ev-1]. This defect was triggered when a flawed content validator allowed an untrusted pointer dereference from a 21-byte input, representing missing input validation and a memory-safety issue [Source: ev-1]. The root cause of such widespread impact was monoculture concentration; one security agent deployed at enterprise scale with fast-cadence updates became a single point of failure for approximately 8.5 million Windows devices worldwide [Source: ev-1].

The consequences were severe across multiple sectors, including airlines, banks, hospitals, broadcasters, and 911 services [Source: ev-1]. Delta Air Lines cancelled roughly 7,000 flights and incurred about $500M in costs before suing CrowdStrike and hiring David Boies [Source: ev-1]. Financially, CrowdStrike shares fell by about 11% on July 22, 2024 following the incident [Source: ev-1]. In response to congressional testimony given by CEO George Kurtz on September 24, 2024, the company apologized and promised deployment-process changes [Source: ev-1]. These changes included moving to staged (phased) rollouts with canary testing and adding sensor-level validation, while the episode also drove industry-wide discussion regarding chaos-engineering and blast-radius limits for endpoint agents in 2024 [Source: ev-1].
