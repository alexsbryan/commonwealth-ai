The United States moved to force the divestiture of TikTok’s US operations or ban it due to national-security concerns, specifically fears that the Chinese government could access US user data and the platform's algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This legal action was formalized when President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on 2024-04-24, which mandated that ByteDance sell its US business within roughly 270 days (with a possible extension of 90 days) or face a prohibition in the country [Source: ev-1].

Following an initial period where the app went dark for approximately 14 hours around 2025-01-18/19 after the Supreme Court upheld the law with a unanimous 9-0 decision on 2025-03-17, President Trump intervened to pause enforcement via an executive order dated 2025-19-01, delaying action by 75 days [Source: ev-1]. The dispute was ultimately resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on 2026-10-06 (noted as 2025 in some contexts but likely referring to mid-2025 negotiations), which structured TikTok's US operations as a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this arrangement, known as "Project Texas," US user data is hosted in Oracle’s cloud under US control, and Oracle acquired approximately a 12.5% stake in the US entity alongside investors like SoftBank, while ByteDance retained a majority interest [Source: ev-1].
