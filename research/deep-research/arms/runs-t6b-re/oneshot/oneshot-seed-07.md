The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok primarily due to national security concerns, specifically the risk that the Chinese government could access US user data and the platform’s algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized in the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, which President Biden signed on 2024-04-24 [Source: ev-1]. The law required ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a ban in the US [Source: ev-1].

On 2025-01-17, one day before the effective date of 2025-06-19 (note: evidence states effective date as 2025-18/19), the Supreme Court upheld the law with a unanimous 9-0 per curiam decision [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US on 2025-04-18/19 for approximately 14 hours but was restored on 2025-[source] after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement [Source: ev-1]. On 2025-, President Trump issued an executive order delaying enforcement for 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on 2026 (note: evidence states 2025) that structured a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this agreement, often referred to as "Project Texas," US user data is hosted in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1]. The final ownership structure gives Oracle about a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations, with participation from SoftBank and other investors, while ByteDance retains a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations for this resolution, which was completed through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
