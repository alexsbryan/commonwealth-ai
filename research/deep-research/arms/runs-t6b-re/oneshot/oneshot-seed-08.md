A US District Court for the District of Columbia found that Google held a monopoly in general search services and general search text advertising, violating Section 2 of the Sherman Act [Source: ev-1]. The court’s theory of harm centered on “default-distribution deals,” where Google paid companies like Apple, Samsung, and Mozilla billions to be their default search engine [Source: ev-1]. Evidence showed that Google’s CEO Sundar Pichai testified the company spent approximately $26.3 billion on distribution in 2021, and trial evidence indicated Google paid Apple roughly 36% of Safari search-ad revenue [Source: ev-1]. Judge Amit Mehta ruled on August 5, 2024, establishing that paying for default placement is exclusionary conduct because it creates a self-reinforcing loop of scale, data, quality, and protection that rivals cannot break [Source: ev-1].

On August 5, 20 appealed to the DC Circuit in 2025, arguing the ruling punished procompetitive innovation; however, the final outcome remained pending through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
