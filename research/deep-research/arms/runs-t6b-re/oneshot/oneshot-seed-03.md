NASA ordered Boeing’s Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA determined that these thrusters could not be guaranteed safe for the critical deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. This safety concern was compounded by the fact that the anomalous thrusters could not be ground-tested in their specific flight condition to verify reliability before re-entry [Source: ev-1].

The decision had significant operational and financial implications for the program. Operationally, astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams were stranded on the International Space Station; they did not return until March 18, 2025, aboard a SpaceX Crew-9 Dragon capsule, resulting in an approximately nine-month stay (roughly 9.5 months) compared to the originally planned eight-day mission duration [Source: ev-1]. Financially and strategically, the incident highlighted severe issues within Boeing’s Commercial Crew contract, which was fixed-price at roughly $4.2 billion since the 2014 program inception—significantly higher than SpaceX's $2.6 billion award [Source: ev-1]. By 2025, Boeing had disclosed cumulative program losses exceeding $1.5 billion [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, NASA announced approximately 400 job cuts in its space division in June 2025, and Starliner’s path toward full certification remained unresolved throughout that year [Source: ev-1].
