NASA ordered Boeing’s Starliner to return uncrewed from its first crewed flight because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA determined that these thrusters could not be guaranteed safe for the critical deorbit burn. This decision was made on August 24, 2024 [Source: ev-1]. The primary technical reason cited was that the affected thrusters could not be ground-tested in their specific flight condition to verify safety [Source: ev-1].

The implications of this decision for the program were significant across operational, financial, and contractual dimensions:
*   **Crew Impact:** Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams, who launched on June 5, 2024, remained aboard the International Space Station until March 18, 2025. They returned via a SpaceX Crew-9 Dragon capsule after spending approximately 9.5 months aloft, compared to the originally planned ~8 days [Source: ev-1].
*   **Spacecraft Disposition:** Starliner undocked from the station on September 6, 2024, and landed at White Sands on September 7, 2024 without its crew [Source: ev-1].
*   **Financial Consequences:** The program operates under a fixed-price contract established in 2014 through NASA’s Commercial Crew program. Boeing had been awarded approximately $4.2 billion for this effort (compared to SpaceX’s roughly $2.6 billion). By 2025, Boeing disclosed cumulative program losses exceeding $1.5 billion [Source: ev-1].
*   **Workforce and Certification:** In June 2025, as part of the fallout from these issues, Boeing announced approximately 400 job cuts within its space division. Furthermore, NASA's path toward certifying Starliner remained unresolved throughout 2025 [Source: ev-1].
