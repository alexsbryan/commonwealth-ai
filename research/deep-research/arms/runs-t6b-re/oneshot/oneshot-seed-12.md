Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because, despite claims of encryption, security researchers demonstrated that its screenshots were stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. This vulnerability meant that sensitive information visible on the screen—such as passwords and private messages—was captured by default and could be accessed by any process running under the user account [Source: ev-1]. The feature was originally announced on May 20, 2024, at Microsoft Build as an AI "memory" tool that screenshotted the display every few seconds for searchable access [Source: ev-1].

In response to the controversy and inquiries from regulators such as the Irish Data Protection Commission, the UK Information Commissioner's Office, and the Washington State Attorney General, Microsoft delayed the release in June 2024 and fundamentally changed how Recall operates [Source: ev-1]. The revised version is now opt-in (default OFF) rather than default-on, requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection to open the timeline, and excludes incognito or DRM content from capture [Source: ev-1]. This updated feature reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
