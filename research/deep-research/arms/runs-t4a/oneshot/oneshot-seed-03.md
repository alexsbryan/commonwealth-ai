NASA ordered Boeing's Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA determined that these anomalies could not be guaranteed safe for the deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. This safety concern was exacerbated by the fact that the thrusters could not be ground-tested in their flight condition [Source: ev-1].

The decision had significant implications for both the astronauts and the program. Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams were stranded on the International Space Station for roughly 9.5 months, a stark contrast to the planned ~8 days of their mission [Source: ev-1]. They eventually returned to Earth aboard a Crew-9 Dragon spacecraft on March 18, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. For the Starliner program itself, which operates under a fixed-price NASA contract from the 2014 Commercial Crew initiative (with Boeing holding approximately $4.2 billion in funding compared to SpaceX's $2.6 billion), the incident contributed to cumulative program losses exceeding $1.5 billion by 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division in June 2025, and as of late 2025, NASA’s certification path for Starliner remained unresolved [Source: ev-1].
