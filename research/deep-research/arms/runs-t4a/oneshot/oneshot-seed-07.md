The United States moved to force TikTok’s sale or ban it primarily due to national security concerns, specifically the risk of Chinese government access to US user data and the platform's algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review and the "Project Texas" initiative, which aimed to host US user data in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legally, President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on April 24, 2024, requiring ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a ban [Source: ev-1]. The Supreme Court upheld this law with a unanimous 9-0 decision on January 17, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US for approximately 14 hours around January 18/19, 2025, before being restored on January 19, 2023, after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement [Source: ev-1]. An executive order issued on January 20, 2025 delayed further enforcement for 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on June 10, 2026 (noted as 2025-06-10 per Bloomberg), which structured the resolution as a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this agreement, Oracle received about a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations, with participation from SoftBank and other investors, while ByteDance retained a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations for this final ownership structure, which remained live throughout the process [Source: ev-1].
