NASA ordered Boeing's Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach on 2024-06-05 [Source: ev-1]. NASA announced this decision on 2024/08/24, stating that these thruster anomalies could not be guaranteed safe for the deorbit burn because they could not be ground-tested in their flight condition [Source: ev-1].

This decision had several significant implications for the program and crew:
*   **Crew Duration:** Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams remained aboard the International Space Station until returning via a Crew-9 Dragon on 2025/03/18 [Source: ev-1]. This resulted in them spending roughly 9.5 months aloft, compared to the planned ~8 days [Source: ev-1].
*   **Spacecraft Recovery:** The Starliner undocked from the station on 2024–09–06 and landed at White Sands on 2024—09—07 without its crew [Source: ev-1].
*   **Financial Impact:** Under the fixed-price NASA contract from the 2014 Commercial Crew program (where Boeing had a budget of about $4.2B versus SpaceX’s $2.6B), Boeing disclosed cumulative program losses of more than $1.5B by 2025 [Source: ev-1].
*   **Operational Consequences:** In June 2025, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division [Source: ev-1], and NASA's Starliner certification path remained unresolved through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
