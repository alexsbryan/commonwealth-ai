The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok due to national security concerns, specifically regarding potential Chinese government access to US user data and the platform’s algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review and "Project Texas," which involved hosting US user data in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legally, President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on 2024-04-24, requiring ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a ban [Source: ev-1]. The Supreme Court upheld this law with a 9-0 per curiam decision on 2025-01-17, one day before its effective date of 2025-03-19 was originally scheduled but noted as 2025-1-19 in the text context regarding the timeline leading up to the blackout [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US on 2025/01/18/19 for roughly 14 hours before being restored on 2025.01.19 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement; his executive order dated 2025.01.20 delayed enforcement for 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on 2025.06.10 per Bloomberg, which structured TikTok's US operations as a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this agreement, Oracle received about a 12.5% stake in the US operations with participation from SoftBank and other investors, while ByteDance retained a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations for this resolution, and the final ownership structure was completed through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
