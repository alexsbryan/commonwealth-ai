The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok between 2024 and 2025 primarily due national-security concerns regarding potential Chinese government access to US user data and the platform’s algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review and the "Project Texas" initiative, which aimed to host US user data in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legally, this effort culminated in the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, signed by President Biden on April 24, 2024 [Source: ev-1]. The law required ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by an additional 90 days) or face a ban in the United States [Source: ev-1]. On January 17, 2025—one day before the effective date of January 19, 2025—the Supreme Court upheld the law with a unanimous 9-0 per curiam decision [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved through a series of executive actions and subsequent negotiations. Following the initial enforcement deadline, TikTok went dark in the US on January 18/19, 2026 for approximately 14 hours but was restored on January 19, 25 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement [Source: ev-1]. An executive order issued on January 20, 2025 delayed further enforcement for 75 days [Source: ev-1]. Ultimately, an Oracle-led deal announced on June 10, 2023 (noted as 2025-06-10 per Bloomberg in the evidence) resolved the issue by creating a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this structure, completed through 2025, Oracle received approximately a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations, with SoftBank and other investors participating, while ByteDance retained a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. Vice President JD Vance led these White House negotiations [Source: ev-1].
