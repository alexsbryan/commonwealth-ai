OpenAI released o3 and o4-mini on April 16, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. The primary motivation for this release was to defend the reasoning-model frontier against competitive pressures from DeepSeek’s R1 cost shock and Anthropic’s agentic-coding push, particularly while GPT-5 had not yet shipped [Source: ev-1].

The launch signaled a specific direction for future frontier reasoning models by reframing them as tool-using systems capable of operating at commodity prices. This shift is evidenced by several key features introduced with these models:
*   **Native Integration:** Vision capabilities were built directly into the model rather than being bolted on, marking the first time OpenAI integrated vision natively in its reasoning models [Source: ev-1].
*   **Trained Tool Use:** The models featured native tool use (such as web search and code interpreter) that was trained into the architecture itself [Source: ev-1].
*   **Dynamic Compute:** They utilized "budget forcing," a technique involving a fixed maximum thought budget but allowing for task-adaptive dynamic compute, meaning harder questions required longer thinking times [Source: ev-1].
*   **Privacy Policies:** It established OpenAI’s first private chain-of-thought policy, ensuring reasoning tokens were not exposed to API callers [Source: ev-1].

Additionally, the launch highlighted a strategy of making advanced capabilities accessible early through an API-first approach. Both o3 and o4-mini were available to developers from day one, although only o4-mini was included in the ChatGPT app at launch; o3 was initially excluded from the consumer app [Source: ev-1]. The pricing structure reinforced this "commodity" positioning, with o3 priced at $10 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, while o4-mini offered lower costs at $1.10 per million input tokens and 4.40 dollars per million output tokens [Source: ev-1].

In terms of performance benchmarks signaling frontier capability, o3 set a record on the ARC-AGI benchmark, achieving approximately 87.5% on ARC-AGI-1 at high compute levels [Source: ev-1]. This score was at or above the ~85% human baseline, making it the first model to reach this threshold [Source: ev-1].
