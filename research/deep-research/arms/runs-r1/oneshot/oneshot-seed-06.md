The global IT outage occurred on 2024-07-19 because a faulty CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor content update triggered an out-of-bounds memory read in the sensor's C++ code [Source: ev-1]. Specifically, a flawed content validator allowed a 21-byte input to pass through, leading to an untrusted pointer dereference and missing input validation that caused Windows machines to crash into blue-screen loops [Source: ev-1]. This incident was exacerbated by "monoculture concentration," where one security agent deployed at enterprise scale with fast-cadence updates became a global single point of failure [Source: ev-1].

The consequences were severe and widespread. Approximately 8.5 million Windows devices were affected globally [Source: ev-1]. The disruption impacted critical sectors including airlines (with Delta cancelling roughly 7,000 flights), banks, hospitals, broadcasters, and 911 services [Source: ev-1]. Financially, Delta incurred about $500M in costs and subsequently sued CrowdStrike while hiring David Boies [Source: ev-1]. In the stock market, CrowdStrike shares fell by approximately 11% on 2024/07/22 [Source: ev-1].

In response to the incident, CEO George Kurtz testified before Congress on 2024–09–24 [Source: ev-1]. The company apologized and promised deployment-process changes, moving toward staged (phased) rollouts with canary testing and adding sensor-level validation [Source: ev-1]. This episode also drove industry-wide discussions regarding chaos-engineering and blast-radius limits for endpoint agents [Source: ev-1].
