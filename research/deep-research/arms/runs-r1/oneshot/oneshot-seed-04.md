OpenAI released o3 and o4-mini on April 16, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. The primary reason for the launch was to defend OpenAI’s position at the frontier of reasoning models against competitive pressures from DeepSeek's R1 cost shock and Anthropic's push into agentic coding [Source: ev-1]. At that time, GPT-5 had not yet shipped, so this release served as a strategic move to maintain leadership in AI capabilities [Source: ev-1].

The launch signaled several key directions for future frontier reasoning models:
*   **Native Integration:** It marked the introduction of the first OpenAI reasoning models with vision built directly into the model itself rather than being bolted on afterward [Source: ev-1].
*   **Tool Use:** The models featured native tool use (including web search and code interpreter) trained into the architecture, reframing frontier capability as "tool-using reasoning" [Source: ev-1].
*   **Cost Efficiency:** By pricing o3 at $10 / $40 per million tokens (input/output) and o4-mini at $1.10 / $4.40, the launch signaled a shift toward making advanced reasoning available at commodity prices [Source: ev-1].
*   **Dynamic Compute:** The technique used was "budget forcing," which employs task-adaptive dynamic compute where harder questions require longer thinking times within a fixed maximum thought budget [Source: ev-1].
*   **Privacy Standards:** It established OpenAI's first private chain-of-thought policy, ensuring that reasoning tokens are not exposed to API callers [Source: ev-1].

Additionally, the release highlighted significant performance benchmarks, with o3 setting a record on ARC-AGI by achieving about 87.5% on ARC-AGI-1 at high compute, reaching or exceeding the ~85% human baseline for the first time among models [Source: ev-1].
