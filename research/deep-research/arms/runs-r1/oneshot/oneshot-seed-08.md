A US court found Google’s search business to be a monopoly because Judge Amit Mehta ruled on 2024-08-05 that the company violated Section 2 of the Sherman Act by acting as a monopolist in general search services and general search text advertising [Source: ev-1]. The theory of harm centered on "default-distribution deals," where Google paid billions to companies like Apple, Samsung, and Mozilla to remain the default search engine [Source: ev-1]. Evidence indicated that Google spent approximately $26.3 billion for distribution in 2021 and paid Apple roughly 36% of Safari search-ad revenue [Source: ev-1]. The court determined this conduct was exclusionary because it created a self-reinforcing loop where defaults generate scale, data, and quality that protect the monopoly position from rivals [Source: ev-1].

The remedies ordered by Judge Mehta on 2025-08-05 included requiring Google to sell Chrome, marking the first forced divestiture of a major tech company since the Microsoft case of 2000 [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, Google is prohibited from paying for default search placement or engaging in revenue-sharing agreements, and faces limits on acquisitions and AI-related investments [Source: ev-1].
