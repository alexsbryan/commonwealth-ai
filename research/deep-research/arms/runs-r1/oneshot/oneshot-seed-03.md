NASA ordered Boeing's Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA determined that these anomalies could not be guaranteed safe for the deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. The decision was made on 2024-08-24 primarily because the affected thrusters could not be ground-tested in their flight condition [Source: ev-1].

This decision had significant consequences for the program. Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams were stranded aboard the International Space Station until they returned on a Crew-9 Dragon on 2025-03-18, resulting in roughly 9.5 months aloft compared to a planned duration of approximately 8 days [Source: ev-1]. Financially and operationally, Boeing disclosed cumulative program losses of more than $1.5 billion by 2025 under its fixed-price contract (valued at about $4.2 billion) from the 2014 Commercial Crew program [Source: ev-1]. Furthermore, the issues led to roughly 400 job cuts in Boeing's space division announced in June 2025, and NASA's certification path for Starliner remained unresolved through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
