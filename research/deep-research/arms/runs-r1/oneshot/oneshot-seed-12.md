Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because, despite claims of encryption, security researchers demonstrated that its screenshots were stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. This storage vulnerability meant that sensitive content visible on the screen—such as passwords and private messages—was captured by default and remained accessible to any process running under the user account [Source: ev-1]. Although Microsoft initially announced the "photographic memory" feature on May 20, 2024, at the Copilot+ PC launch, these security flaws led them to delay the rollout in June 2024 [Source: ev-1].

In response to the controversy and inquiries from regulators including the Irish Data Protection Commission, the UK Information Commissioner's Office, and an investigation opened by Washington State Attorney General Bob Ferguson, Microsoft fundamentally changed how Recall operates [Source: ev-1]. The revised version is now opt-in (default OFF) rather than default-on, requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection to access the timeline, and explicitly excludes incognito or DRM content [Source: ev-1]. This updated, privacy-focused iteration reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
