Anthropic released the Claude 4 family in May 2025 as two distinct models—Claude Opus 4 and Claude Sonnet 4—to serve different market needs: Opus 4 positioned as the flagship model, while Sonnet 4 was designed as a fast workhorse [Source: ev-1]. The launch occurred on May 8, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. This dual-model strategy allowed Anthropic to offer tiered pricing, with Opus 4 costing $15 per million input tokens and $75 per million output tokens, whereas Sonnet 4 was priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens [Source: ev-1]. By setting these specific price points, particularly for Sonnet 4, Anthropic established it as the developer-default coding model [Source: ev-1].

The release signaled that agentic coding had become a defining product category in 2025, driven largely by the rise of Claude Code [Source: ev-1]. The launch highlighted "computer use 2.0" (browser control) capabilities that were roughly twice as reliable as those of the previous generation, marking a significant leap in reliability over version 3.x models [Source: ev-1]. This move positioned Anthropic against competitors like OpenAI’s o-series during an enterprise agentic-coding gold rush, with Anthropic marketing its products as the safest and most reliable coding agents [Source: ev-1]. The market response was strong, resulting in record API usage for Anthropic [Source: ev-1].

Subsequent developments reinforced this positioning; a June 18, 2025 update to version 4.1 improved coding and agentic reliability without changing prices [Source: ev-1]. Although there were internal disputes regarding naming conventions (choosing "4.1" over "4.5"), the commercial impact remained significant [Source: ev-1]. By late September 2025, sustained adoption of Claude Code led Anthropic to raise funding at a reported valuation of $183 billion, cementing its status as the leader in the coding-agent market [Source: ev-1].
