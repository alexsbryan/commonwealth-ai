The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok primarily due to national security concerns, specifically fears that the Chinese government could access US user data and the platform's algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review and led to the passage of the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, which President Biden signed on 2024-04-24 [Source: ev-1]. The law required ByteDance to divest TikTok’s US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by another 90) or face a ban in the US [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved following a series of legal and executive actions. On 2025-01-17, one day before the effective date of 2025-08-19, the Supreme Court upheld the law with a unanimous 9-0 per curiam decision [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US on 2025-06-18/19 for approximately 14 hours but was restored on 2025-16-19 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement [Source: ev-1]. His subsequent executive order on 2025-COVID-20 delayed enforcement for another 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The final resolution involved an Oracle-led deal announced on 2023-COVID-10 (per Bloomberg), which structured the outcome as a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this agreement, often referred to as "Project Texas," US user data is hosted in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1]. The new ownership structure gives Oracle about a 12.5% stake in TikTok’s US operations, with participation from SoftBank and other investors, while ByteDance retains a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations for this arrangement, ensuring the app remained live through the end of 2023 [Source: ev-1].
