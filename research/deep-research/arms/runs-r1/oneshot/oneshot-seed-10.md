OpenAI released GPT-5 on August 7, 2025 [Source: ev-1], marking a strategic shift to consolidate its product lineup and compete through distribution rather than just benchmark performance. The release ended the separation between reasoning and non-reasoning models by folding previous o-series products (o3, o4-mini, o3-mini, and o3-pro) into GPT-5’s internal "reasoning modes" [Source: ev-1]. This change positioned GPT-5 as a single unified model that "thinks," eliminating the need for users to select different models based on task complexity [Source: ev-1].

Regarding how OpenAI sells its models, this launch represented a deliberate move toward aggressive free-tier distribution and price collapse [Source: ev-1]. Key changes include:
*   **Pricing Structure:** The company adopted a "give-away-the-frontier pricing" strategy where GPT-5 is available immediately to free users [Source: ev-1]. Paid tiers are structured with Plus at $20/month (offering unlimited access), Pro at $200/month, and API costs set at $1.25 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens for the main model, while GPT-5 mini is priced at $0.25 per million input and $2 per million output [Source: ev-1].
*   **Competitive Focus:** The competitive battleground shifted from benchmark demos to "default agency," focusing on models that act rather than just answer questions [Source: ev-1].

This strategy was supported by significant technical features, including a 5-million-token context window, native image generation without separate models, integrated deep research, and built-in file upload and web search capabilities [Source: ev-1]. Despite these changes, performance remained strong, with GPT-5 scoring approximately 78.6% on SWE-bench Verified at prices significantly lower than the retired o-series products [Source: ev-1].
