The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok between 2024 and 2025 primarily due to national security concerns regarding potential Chinese government access to US user data and the platform’s algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review mechanisms, including the "Project Texas" hosting initiative which aimed to keep US user data in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legislatively, President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on April 24, 2024 [Source: ev-1]. The law required ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (with a possible extension of 90 days) or face a ban in the United States [Source: ev-1]. On January 17, 2025—one day before the effective date of January 19, 2025—the Supreme Court upheld the law with a unanimous 9-0 per curiam decision [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved through an interim pause and a subsequent joint venture structure. Following the Supreme Court ruling, TikTok went dark in the US on January 18/19, 2020 for approximately 14 hours before being restored on January 19 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement [Source: ev-1]. An executive order issued by President Trump on January 20 delayed enforcement for 75 days [Source: ev-1]. The final resolution involved an Oracle-led deal announced on June 10, 2025, which gave Oracle about a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations with participation from SoftBank and other investors [Source: ev-1]. In this arrangement, Vice President JD Vance led White House negotiations, ByteDance retained a majority interest, and the app remained live as part of a joint venture rather than an outright sale, completing the ownership structure through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
