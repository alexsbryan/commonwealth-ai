DeepSeek's R1 release triggered the largest single-day loss in Nvidia’s history because it demonstrated that a frontier-class reasoning model could be trained at a fraction of incumbent costs, thereby challenging the market thesis that AI capital expenditure and demand for Nvidia chips are protected by compute scale [Source: ev-1]. Specifically, DeepSeek released its open-weights reasoning model, DeepSeek-R1, on January 20, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. This followed the release of its predecessor, DeepSeek-V3, on December 26, 2024, which reported training on roughly 2.79 million GPU-hours at a claimed cost of about US$5.6 million using export-restricted H800 chips [Source: ev-1].

The event revealed two key aspects regarding frontier-AI training economics and market dynamics:
1. **Cost Efficiency via Scarcity:** The low training costs were driven by efficiency improvements necessitated by US export controls (specifically H800 restrictions), meaning scarcity rather than preference drove these economic outcomes [Source: ev-1].
2. **Market Repricing:** On January 27, 2025, Nvidia lost approximately US$589 billion in market capitalization—the largest single-day loss in stock-market history—with about US$1 trillion erased across the tech complex as markets repriced the value of compute-scale moats [Source: ev-1].
