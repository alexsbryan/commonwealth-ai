Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because it was initially designed as an AI "memory" tool that took screenshots of the user's screen every few seconds and stored them locally [Source: ev-1]. Although Microsoft claimed these files were encrypted, security researchers demonstrated that they were actually stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. This allowed any process running under the user account to access sensitive information captured by default, including passwords, health data, and private messages [Source: ev-1]. The discrepancy between the security claims and the actual implementation, combined with the default-on nature of recording everything on screen, led privacy regulators such as the Irish Data Protection Commission, the UK Information Commissioner’s Office, and the Washington State Attorney General to raise questions or open investigations [Source: ev-1].

In response to this controversy, Microsoft delayed the feature in June 2024 and subsequently reversed its default settings [Source: ev-1]. The relaunched version is now opt-in (default OFF) rather than enabled by default [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, accessing the timeline now requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection, while incognito mode and DRM content are explicitly excluded from capture [Source: ev-1]. While the revised opt-in Recall reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025, the initial incident served as a major case study for shipping AI features with privacy risks by default [Source: ev-1].
