NASA ordered Boeing's Starliner to return uncrewed from its first crewed flight because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during the docking approach, and NASA determined that these anomalies could not be guaranteed safe for the deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. Specifically, the decision was driven by the fact that the thrusters could not be ground-tested in their specific flight condition to ensure safety [Source: ev-1].

This decision had significant implications for both the astronauts and the program itself. Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams were stranded on the International Space Station for roughly 9.5 months, a duration vastly exceeding their planned ~8-day mission, before they finally returned aboard a Crew-9 Dragon spacecraft on March 18, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. For the program, the incident highlighted severe financial and operational challenges; under Boeing's fixed-price contract from the 2014 Commercial Crew Program (valued at approximately $4.2 billion compared to SpaceX’s $2.6 billion), Boeing disclosed cumulative program losses of more than $1.5 billion by 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, in June 2025, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division, and as of that time, NASA's path toward certifying Starliner remained unresolved [Source: ev-1].
