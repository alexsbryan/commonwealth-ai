Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because, despite claims that data was encrypted, security researchers demonstrated that the screenshots it captured were stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. This storage vulnerability meant that sensitive content visible on screen—such as passwords and private messages—was accessible to any process running under the user account by default [Source: ev-1]. The episode highlighted a collision between the feature's value proposition of "photographic memory" and standard privacy expectations for local data, leading regulatory bodies like the Irish Data Protection Commission and the UK Information Commissioner's Office to raise questions and prompting an investigation by the Washington State Attorney General [Source: ev-1].

To address these concerns, Microsoft delayed the initial launch in June 2024 and subsequently reversed its configuration strategy [Source: ev-1]. The relaunched feature is now opt-in (default OFF) rather than enabled automatically [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, accessing the timeline now requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection, while incognito and DRM content are explicitly excluded from capture [Source: ev-1]. This revised version of Recall reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
