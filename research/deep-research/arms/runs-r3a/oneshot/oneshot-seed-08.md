A US court found Google’s search business to be a monopoly because it violated Section 2 of the Sherman Act by engaging in exclusionary conduct through default-distribution deals [Source: ev-1]. Specifically, Judge Amit Mehta ruled on August 5, 2024, that Google was a monopolist in general search services and general search text advertising after paying companies like Apple, Samsung, and Mozilla billions of dollars to remain their default search engine [Source: ev-1]. Evidence showed that Google paid approximately $26.3 billion for distribution in 2021, including roughly 36% of Safari search-ad revenue to Apple [Source: ev-1]. The court determined this created a self-reinforcing loop where defaults generated scale, data, and quality advantages that protected the monopoly position against rivals [Source: ev-1].

The remedies ordered by Judge Mehta on August 5, 2 025 included structural changes requiring Google to sell its Chrome browser, marking the first forced divestiture of a major tech company since the Microsoft case of 2000 [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, Google was prohibited from paying for default search placement or entering into revenue-sharing agreements, and it faces limits on acquisitions and AI-related investments [Source: ev-1].
