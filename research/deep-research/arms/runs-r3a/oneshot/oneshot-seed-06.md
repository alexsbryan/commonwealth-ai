The global IT outage in July 2024 was caused by a faulty CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor content update, specifically a "Rapid Response Content" channel file that contained an out-of-bounds memory read defect [Source: ev-1]. This technical failure resulted from missing input validation and a flawed content validator, which allowed untrusted pointer dereference triggered by a small 21-byte input to crash the software [Source: ev-1]. The root cause of such widespread impact was identified as monoculture concentration; because one security agent had been deployed at enterprise scale with fast-cadence updates, it became a single point of failure for global infrastructure [Source: ev-1].

The consequences were severe and far-reaching. On July 19, 2024, approximately 8.5 million Windows devices worldwide crashed into blue-screen loops due to this update [Source: ev-1]. Critical sectors including airlines, banks, hospitals, broadcasters, and emergency services (including 911) suffered significant disruptions [Source: ev-1]. For example, Delta Air Lines cancelled roughly 7,000 flights and incurred about $500M in costs, leading the company to sue CrowdStrike and hire David Boies as legal counsel [Source: ev-1]. Financially, CrowdStrike shares fell by about 11% on July 22, 2024 [Source: ev-1]. In response, CEO George Kurtz testified before Congress on September 24, 2024; the company apologized and promised deployment-process changes, such as moving to staged rollouts with canary testing and adding sensor-level validation [Source: ev-1].
