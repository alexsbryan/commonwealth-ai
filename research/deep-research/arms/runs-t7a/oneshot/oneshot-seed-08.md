Based on the evidence provided, Judge Amit Mehta of the US District Court for the District of Columbia ruled that Google is a monopolist in general search services and general search text advertising because it violated Section 2 of the Sherman Act [Source: ev-1]. The court's theory of harm centered on "default-distribution deals," where Google paid companies such as Apple, Samsung, and Mozilla billions to be their default search engine [Source: ev-1]. Evidence indicated that Google’s CEO Sundar Pichai testified the company spent approximately $26.3 billion on distribution in 2021, with trial data showing Google paid Apple roughly 36% of Safari search-ad revenue [Source: ev-1]. This conduct was deemed exclusionary because paying for default placement creates a self-reinforcing loop—defaults generate scale, which generates data, which improves quality, thereby protecting the default status against rivals [Source: ev-1].

Regarding remedies, Judge Mehta ordered structural changes on August 5, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. The court mandated that Google must sell Chrome, marking the first forced divestiture of a major tech company since the Microsoft case of 2000 [Source: ev-1]. Additionally, Google is prohibited from paying for default search placement (banning revenue-sharing agreements) and faces limits on acquisitions and AI-related investments [Source: ev-1]. Although Google appealed to the DC Circuit in 2025 arguing the ruling punished procompetitive innovation, the final outcome remained pending through 2025 following the initial liability ruling issued on August 5, 24 [Source: ev-1]
