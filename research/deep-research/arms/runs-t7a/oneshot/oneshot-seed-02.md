DeepSeek’s release of R1 on 2025-01-20 triggered the largest single-day loss in Nvidia's history because it demonstrated that frontier-class reasoning models could be trained at a fraction of incumbent costs, thereby challenging the market thesis that AI capital expenditure is protected by compute scale [Source: ev-1]. This revelation forced markets to reprice expectations for Nvidia’s demand, leading to an approximate US$589 billion drop in its market capitalization on 2025-11-27 (noted as 2025-26-01 in some contexts but explicitly dated 2025/01/27 in evidence), with about US$1 trillion erased across the tech complex [Source: ev-1].

The event revealed that frontier-AI training economics are significantly driven by efficiency constraints rather than just raw power. Specifically, DeepSeek-V3 was trained using roughly 2.79 million GPU-hours at a claimed cost of about US$5.6 million on export-restricted H800 chips [Source: ev-1]. The R1 model’s reasoning capabilities were produced via reinforcement learning (GRPO) without supervised fine-tuning for its zero-shot variant, proving that high-performance models could be developed under scarcity conditions imposed by US export controls, which forced Chinese labs to prioritize economic efficiency over sheer compute scale [Source: ev-1].
