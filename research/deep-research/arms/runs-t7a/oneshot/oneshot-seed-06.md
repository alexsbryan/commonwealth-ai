On July 19, 2024 [Source: ev-1], a faulty CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor content update caused a global IT outage by crashing Windows machines in blue-screen loops [Source: ev-1]. The root cause was an out-of-bounds memory read in the sensor's C++ code, specifically triggered by a flawed content validator that allowed a 21-byte input to pass and trigger an untrusted pointer dereference [Source: ev-1]. This incident affected approximately 8.5 million Windows devices worldwide [Source: ev-1].

The consequences of the outage were severe across multiple sectors. Airlines grounded their fleets, with Delta cancelling roughly 7,000 flights and incurring about $500M in costs before suing CrowdStrike [Source: ev-1]. Other disrupted services included banks, hospitals, broadcasters, and 911 emergency lines [Source: ev-1]. Financially, CrowdStrike shares fell by about 11% on July 22, 2024, following the incident [Source: ev-1]. In response to regulatory pressure, CEO George Kurtz testified before Congress on September 24, 2024; the company apologized and implemented changes such as staged rollouts with canary testing and added sensor-level validation to prevent similar single points of failure [Source: ev-1].
