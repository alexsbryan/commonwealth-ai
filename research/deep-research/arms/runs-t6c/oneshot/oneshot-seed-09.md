Anthropic released the Claude 4 family in May 2025 as two distinct models—Claude Opus 4 and Claude Sonnet 4—to address different market needs within a single product line. Specifically, Opus 4 was positioned as the flagship model, while Sonnet 4 served as the "fast workhorse" [Source: ev-1]. This dual-model strategy allowed Anthropic to offer tiered pricing that targeted both high-end performance and cost-effective development; Opus 4 was priced at $15 per million input tokens and $75 per million output tokens, whereas Sonnet 4 was significantly cheaper at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens [Source: ev-1]. By setting this lower price point for Sonnet 4, Anthropic explicitly designed it to be the developer-default coding model [Source: ev-1].

The launch signaled a major shift in the agentic-coding market toward reliability and enterprise adoption. The headline capability of the release was "computer use 2.0," which offered browser control roughly twice as reliable as the previous generation's capabilities [Source: ev-1]. This improvement positioned Claude 4 as the safest and most reliable coding agent against direct competitors like OpenAI’s o-series during what is described as an "enterprise agentic-coding gold rush" [Source: ev-1]. Furthermore, the timing coincided with the rise of Claude Code as a defining product for 2025, leading to record API usage [Source: ev-1]. Ultimately, this launch cemented Anthropic’s position as a leader in the coding-agent space, evidenced by its subsequent large funding round at a reported $183 billion valuation driven largely by Claude Code adoption [Source: ev-1].
