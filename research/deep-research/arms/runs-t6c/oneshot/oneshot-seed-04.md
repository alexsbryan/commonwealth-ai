OpenAI released o3 and o4-mini on April 16, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. The launch signaled a strategic shift in the direction of frontier reasoning models toward "tool-using reasoning at commodity prices" [Source: ev-1]. Specifically, these were OpenAI's first reasoning models with vision built directly into the model architecture rather than bolted on, and they featured native tool use (such as web search and code interpreter) trained into the model itself [Source: ev-1].

The release was designed to defend the reasoning-model frontier against competitive pressures from DeepSeek’s R1 cost shock and Anthropic’s agentic-coding push [Source: ev-1]. By offering o3 at $10 per million input tokens / $40 per million output tokens, and o4-mini at $1.10 / $4.40 respectively, OpenAI positioned these advanced capabilities as accessible via API-first availability starting day one for developers [Source: ev-1]. This approach emphasized a new technique called "budget forcing," which utilizes task-adaptive dynamic compute with a fixed maximum thought budget, allowing harder questions to require longer thinking times while maintaining private chain-of-thought policies where reasoning tokens are not exposed to API callers [Source: ev-1].

Furthermore, the launch highlighted significant performance milestones; o3 set a record on the ARC-AGI benchmark by achieving approximately 87.5% on ARC-AGI-1 at high compute, reaching or exceeding the ~85% human baseline and becoming the first model to do so [Source: ev-1]. Although GPT-5 was not yet shipping at this time, OpenAI used the April 2025 release of o3 (which was initially absent from the ChatGPT app) and o4-mini (available in the app) to reframe frontier capability standards for the industry [Source: ev-1].
