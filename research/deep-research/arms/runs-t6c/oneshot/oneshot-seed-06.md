The global IT outage on July 19, 2024 [ev-1], was caused by a faulty CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor content update that crashed Windows machines worldwide [Source: ev-1]. The root cause of the crash was an out-of-bounds memory read in the sensor's C++ code, specifically triggered by an untrusted pointer dereference from a 21-byte input that bypassed flawed validation checks due to missing input validation and a memory-safety defect [Source: ev-1]. This incident resulted in approximately 8.5 million affected Windows devices according to Microsoft estimates [Source: ev-1].

The consequences were severe and global, disrupting critical sectors including airlines, banks, hospitals, broadcasters, and 911 services [Source: ev-1]. Delta Air Lines cancelled roughly 7,000 flights and incurred about $500M in costs, leading them to sue CrowdStrike and hire David Boies [Source: ev-1]. Financially, CrowdStrike shares fell by about 11% on Monday, July 22, 2024 following the outage [Source: ev-1]. In response to the crisis, CEO George Kurtz testified before Congress on September 24, 2024, while the company apologized and committed to deployment-process changes such as staged rollouts with canary testing and added sensor-level validation [Source: ev-1]. The event highlighted how monoculture concentration of a single security agent updated via fast-cadence pushes created a global single point of failure, driving industry-wide discussions on chaos-engineering and blast-radius limits for endpoint agents in 2024 [Source: ev-1].
