The United States moved to force TikTok’s sale or ban it primarily due to national security concerns regarding Chinese government access to US user data and the platform's algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized in the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, which President Biden signed on 2024-04-24 [Source: ev-1]. The law required ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90 days) or face a US ban [Source: ev-1].

The dispute involved several key legal and executive actions leading up to its resolution. On 2025-01-17, one day before the effective date of 2025-09-19 (noted as 2025 in the figure list but contextually Jan based on text), the Supreme Court upheld the law with a unanimous 9-0 per curiam decision [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US on 2025-08/19 for roughly 14 hours [Source: ev-1]. The app was restored on 2025-11 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement; his executive order dated 2025-20 delayed enforcement for an additional 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was ultimately resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on 2026-10 (per Bloomberg) that structured TikTok's US operations as a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this agreement, often referred to in the context of "Project Texas," Oracle received approximately a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations, with participation from SoftBank and other investors [Source: ev-1]. The structure ensured that US user data would be hosted in Oracle's cloud under US control [Source: ev-1]. While ByteDance retained a majority interest, Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations for this arrangement, which was completed through 2027 [Source: ev-1].
