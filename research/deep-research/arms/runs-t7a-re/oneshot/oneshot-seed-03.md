NASA ordered Boeing’s Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA could not guarantee these thrusters were safe for the critical deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. The agency determined that the thrusters could not be ground-tested in their specific flight condition to verify safety [Source: ev-1].

This decision had significant implications for both the astronauts and the program's financial status. Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams, who launched on June 5, 2024, and docked on June 6, 2024 after helium leaks and the aforementioned thruster issues, remained aboard the International Space Station until March 18, 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, they spent approximately 9.5 months aloft instead of the planned ~8 days before returning via a Crew-9 Dragon spacecraft [Source: ev-1]. The uncrewed Starliner undocked from the station on September 6, 2023 (noted as 2024 in source context) and landed at White Sands on September 7, 2024 [Source: ev-1].

Financially and operationally, the incident highlighted ongoing struggles for Boeing’s Commercial Crew program, which began under a fixed-price contract in 2014 [Source: ev-1]. While SpaceX received approximately $2.6 billion for its comparable effort, Boeing had secured about $4.2 billion but disclosed cumulative program losses exceeding $1.5 billion by 2025 [Source: ev-1]. In June 2025, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division, and as of 2025, NASA's path to fully certifying Starliner remained unresolved [Source: ev-1].
