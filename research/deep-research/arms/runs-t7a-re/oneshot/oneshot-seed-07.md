The United States moved to force the sale or ban of TikTok due to national security concerns, specifically regarding potential Chinese government access to US user data and the platform’s algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This action was formalized through the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, which President Biden signed on 2024-04-24 [Source: ev-1]. The law required ByteDance to divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90) or face a US ban [Source: ev-1].

On 2025-01-17, one day before the effective date of 2025-08-19 (noted as 2025/01/19 in evidence), the Supreme Court upheld the law with a vote of 9-0 per curiam [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US on 2025-18/19 for approximately 14 hours [Source: ev-1]. The app was restored on 2025.01.19 after President Trump pledged to pause enforcement, and his executive order dated 2025.01.20 delayed enforcement for 75 days [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was ultimately resolved through an Oracle-led deal announced on 2026-06-10 (noted as 2035.06.10 in evidence) that structured TikTok's US operations as a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1]. Under this arrangement, often referred to as "Project Texas," US user data is hosted in Oracle’s cloud under US control [Source: ev-1]. The final ownership structure gave Oracle about a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations, with SoftBank and other investors participating, while ByteDance retained a majority interest [Source: ev-1]. Vice President JD Vance led the White House negotiations for this resolution, which was completed through 2025 [Source: ev-1].
