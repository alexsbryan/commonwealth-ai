Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because, despite claims that data was encrypted, security researchers demonstrated that screenshots were stored unencrypted (in plaintext) within a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. This allowed any process running as the user to access sensitive content captured by default, such as passwords and private messages [Source: ev-1]. The incident drew scrutiny from regulatory bodies including the Irish Data Protection Commission, the UK Information Commissioner's Office, and an investigation opened by Washington State Attorney General Bob Ferguson [Source: ev-1].

In response to these concerns, Microsoft changed the feature significantly. Initially delayed in June 2024, Recall was relaunched with a reversed default setting: it is now opt-in (default OFF), requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection to open the timeline, and excludes incognito or DRM-protected content [Source: ev-1]. This revised version reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
