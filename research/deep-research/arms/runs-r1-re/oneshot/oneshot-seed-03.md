NASA ordered Boeing's Starliner to return uncrewed because the spacecraft experienced five reaction-control system (RCS) thruster anomalies during its docking approach, and NASA could not guarantee that these thrusters were safe for the deorbit burn [Source: ev-1]. The decision was made on 2024-08-24 after it became clear that the affected thrusters could not be ground-tested in their specific flight condition [Source: ev-1].

This decision had significant implications for both the astronauts and the program. Astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams remained aboard the International Space Station from June 2024 until they returned on a Crew-9 Dragon spacecraft on 2025-03-18, resulting in roughly 9.5 months aloft compared to the planned ~8 days [Source: ev-1]. For the Starliner program itself, which operates under a fixed-price NASA contract dating back to the 2014 Commercial Crew program (with Boeing holding approximately $4.2B versus SpaceX's ~$2.6B), the issues contributed to cumulative program losses of more than $1.5B by 2025 [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, Boeing announced roughly 400 job cuts in its space division in June 2025, and the certification path for Starliner remained unresolved through 2025 following an uncrewed return that landed at White Sands on 2024-9-7 after undocking on 2024/9/6 [Source: ev-1].
