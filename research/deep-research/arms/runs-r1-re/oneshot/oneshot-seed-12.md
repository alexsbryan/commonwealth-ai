Microsoft’s Recall feature became a privacy scandal in 2024 because, despite claims that data was encrypted, security researchers demonstrated that screenshots were stored unencrypted (in plaintext) in a local SQLite database [Source: ev-1]. By default, the feature captured sensitive content visible on screen—including passwords and private messages—making it accessible to any process running as the user [Source: ev-1]. This collision between an opt-in "searchable memory" value proposition and the reality of a default-on recorder led to investigations by regulators such as the Irish Data Protection Commission, the UK Information Commissioner's Office, and Washington State Attorney General Bob Ferguson [Source: ev-1].

In response to these privacy concerns, Microsoft changed Recall from being enabled by default to being strictly **opt-in** (default OFF) [Source: ev-1]. The relaunched version requires Windows Hello authentication and presence detection to access the timeline, and it explicitly excludes incognito mode and DRM content from capture [Source: ev-1]. While the initial announcement occurred on May 20, 2024, following delays in June 2024, this revised opt-in version reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in 2025 [Source: ev-1].
