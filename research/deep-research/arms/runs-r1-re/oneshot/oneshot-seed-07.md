The United States moved to force the divestiture or ban of TikTok due to national-security concerns, specifically the potential for Chinese government access to US user data and the platform's algorithm [Source: ev-1]. This concern was formalized through CFIUS review and the implementation of "Project Texas," which required hosting US user data in Oracle’s cloud under US control [Source: ev-1].

Legislative action began when President Biden signed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act on 2024-04-24 [Source: ev-1]. The law mandated that ByteDance divest TikTok's US operations within roughly 270 days (extendable by 90 days) or face a ban in the United States [Source: ev-1]. On 2025-01-17, one day before the effective date of 2025-0The Supreme Court upheld the law with a unanimous 9-0 per curiam decision [Source: ev-1]. Consequently, TikTok went dark in the US on 2025-0Jan 18/19 for approximately 14 hours [Source: ev-1].

The dispute was resolved when President Trump pledged to pause enforcement, restoring service on 2025-Jan 19 and issuing an executive order on 2025-Ja 20 that delayed enforcement for another 75 days [Source: ev-1]. The final resolution involved an Oracle-led deal announced on 2025-Jun 10, where Oracle acquired about a 12.5% stake in TikTok's US operations alongside SoftBank and other investors [Source: ev-1]. Led by Vice President JD Vance’s negotiations at the White House, ByteDance retained a majority interest, structuring the outcome as a joint venture rather than an outright sale [Source: ev-1].
