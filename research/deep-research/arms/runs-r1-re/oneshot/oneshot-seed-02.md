DeepSeek’s R1 release triggered the largest single-day loss in Nvidia’s history because it demonstrated that a frontier-class, open-weights reasoning model could be trained at a fraction of the cost previously assumed necessary for such capabilities. Specifically, DeepSeek released R1 on 2025-01-20 [Source: ev-1]. Its predecessor, DeepSeek-V3 (released 2024-12-26), reported training on roughly 2.79 million GPU-hours at a claimed cost of about US$5.6 million using export-restricted H800 chips [Source: ev-1]. This low-cost efficiency challenged the market thesis that demand for Nvidia’s high-end compute is moated by scale and exclusive access to expensive hardware.

Consequently, on 2025-1-27, Nvidia lost approximately US$589 billion in market capitalization, marking the largest single-day loss in stock-market history at the time, with about US$1 trillion erased across the tech complex [Source: ev-1]. The event revealed that frontier-AI training economics are significantly driven by efficiency rather than just raw compute expenditure; specifically, it showed that scarcity constraints (such as US export controls forcing Chinese labs toward H800 usage) can drive economic efficiencies that undercut incumbent cost structures [Source: ev-1].
