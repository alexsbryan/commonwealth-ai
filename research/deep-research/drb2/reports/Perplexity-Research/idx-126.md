Overview of Research Findings on 24-Hour Movement Guidelines and
Overweight/Obesity in School-Aged Children and Adolescents
Overview of Research Findings
Research examining adherence to 24-hour Movement Guidelines and overweight/obesity risk in
school-aged children and adolescents (ages 5-18, excluding preschoolers) reveals substantial
evidence supporting a protective association between meeting these integrated guidelines and
reduced obesity risk. Based on systematic review data and individual studies published through
May 2023, the evidence demonstrates clear patterns:
Studies Finding a Correlation:
Approximately 8 major individual studies and multiple meta-analyses documented significant
associations between adherence to 24-hour Movement Guidelines and lower overweight/obesity
risk in school-aged children and adolescents:
   Roman-Viñas et al. (2016) across 12 countries found that children meeting all three
   recommendations had substantially lower odds of obesity (OR = 0.28, 95% CI 0.18-0.45)
   compared to those meeting none [1]
   Carson et al. (2017) in Canada demonstrated a clear dose-response relationship, where
   meeting fewer recommendations was progressively associated with higher BMI z-scores
   and waist circumference [2]
   Katzmarzyk et al. (2017) in the USA showed significant associations between the number of
   guidelines met and multiple obesity indicators including BMI, visceral adipose tissue, and
   cardiometabolic risk factors [3]
   Zhu et al. (2020) in a US nationally representative sample of 30,478 adolescents found that
   meeting zero guidelines versus all three was associated with 4.25-fold higher odds of
   obesity (95% CI 2.87-6.31) [4]
   Tanaka et al. (2020) in Japan demonstrated that multiple guideline combinations were
   associated with lower odds of overweight/obesity, with the most consistent findings for
   screen time recommendations [5]
   Chen et al. (2020/2021) in China examined 114,072 children and adolescents, finding that
   those meeting guidelines showed significantly lower odds of overweight/obesity, with
   stronger effects in younger age groups [6]
   Chemtob et al. (2021) demonstrated longitudinal associations, where children meeting fewer
   guideline components in childhood had substantially higher adiposity measures 2 and 7
   years later, with a 1.66 standard deviation difference in BMI z-score [7]
   Leppänen et al. (2022) in Finland showed that meeting physical activity guidelines,
   particularly in combination with other recommendations, was longitudinally associated with
   lower cardiometabolic risk and waist circumference [8]
   López-Gil et al. (2023) meta-analysis found cross-sectional associations between meeting
   all recommendations and lower obesity indicators (OR = 0.65 for overweight/obesity, 95%
   CI: 0.56-0.76; OR = 0.28 for obesity alone, 95% CI: 0.16-0.50), though no longitudinal
   associations emerged [9]
   Nagata et al. (2023) demonstrated that the combination of low screen time and high
   physical activity was most protective, with high screen time substantially increasing obesity
   risk regardless of activity level [10]
   García-Hermoso et al. (2023) in a 14-year longitudinal follow-up found that meeting screen
   time and physical activity recommendations during adolescence was associated with lower
   risk of abdominal obesity in adulthood (IRR = 0.86, 95% CI 0.67-0.97) [11]
Studies Finding No Correlation:
Systematic review evidence (Marques et al., 2023) identified that among school-aged children
and adolescents studies, approximately 6 out of 15 found no significant associations between
meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and obesity/overweight indicators, and 1 study showed
mixed results depending on age stratification. However, these studies were less frequently
represented in the literature compared to those reporting positive associations. [12]
Key Specific Conclusions from Studies Finding Correlations:
The research consistently demonstrated that:
 1. Dose-response relationships: More guidelines met was associated with progressively
    lower obesity risk [2] [3]
 2. Physical activity primacy: The moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) component
    was most consistently associated with lower obesity risk, even when analyzed
    independently [4] [1]
 3. Combined effects most protective: Meeting all three recommendations (MVPA ≥60
    min/day, screen time ≤2 h/day, adequate sleep) consistently showed the strongest
    protective effects, though meeting individual recommendations also demonstrated
    protective associations [7] [10]
 4. Longitudinal evidence: Studies with follow-up periods demonstrated that non-adherence in
    earlier childhood predicted higher adiposity in later years [11] [8] [7]
 5. Population consistency: Protective associations were observed across diverse geographic
    regions (USA, Canada, China, Japan, Finland, Australia) and 12-country international
    samples, suggesting generalizability [6] [10] [1] [4]
 6. Measurement variations: Associations were consistent across different obesity indicators
    including BMI, BMI z-score, waist circumference, body fat percentage, and visceral
    adiposity [3] [1] [2]
Important Limitations:
   The López-Gil meta-analysis found cross-sectional associations but notably no significant
   longitudinal associations [9]
   Effect sizes, while statistically significant, were often modest in magnitude
   Very low adherence rates globally (typically 5-17% meeting all three recommendations)
   limited ability to study high adherence populations [1] [4] [6]
