# Video Activity Recognition: State-of-the-Art Survey (Up to Mid-2019)

## Technical Survey

### Table 1: Hand-Crafted Motion Features Methods

| Method (Citation) | Year | Core Technique | Dataset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sparse Spatio-Temporal Features (Dollár et al.) [1] | 2005 | Space-time interest point detection using cuboids; sparse features combined with bag-of-words encoding | KTH, Weizmann |
| Dense Trajectories (Wang & Schmid) [2] | 2011 | Dense tracking of sampled points using optical flow; extraction of HOG, HOF, and MBH descriptors along trajectories | UCF101, HMDB51, Hollywood |
| Action Recognition with Improved Trajectories (Wang & Schmid) [3] | 2013 | Improved Dense Trajectories accounting for camera motion; Fisher Vector encoding for feature aggregation | UCF101, HMDB51, THUMOS14, ActivityNet |
| Trajectory-Pooled Deep Convolutional Networks (Wang et al.) [4] | 2014 | Combination of improved dense trajectories with deep convolutional features; spatial CNN + temporal CNN fusion | UCF101, HMDB51 |
| Histogram of Oriented Displacements (Gowayyed et al.) [5] | 2013 | HOD descriptor capturing local motion information from trajectory displacements with multi-level temporal pyramid | Action3D dataset |
| Behavior Recognition via Sparse Spatio-Temporal Features (Dollár et al.) [1] | 2005 | Sparse space-time interest points detected using separable linear filters; cuboid-based feature extraction | Weizmann, KTH |
| Fisher Vector encoding with Dense Trajectories (Oneata et al.) [6] | 2013 | Dense trajectory features (MBH, SIFT, MFCC) encoded using Fisher Vector representation; linear SVM classification | HMDB51, UCF101, Hollywood |

### Table 2: Depth Information-Based Methods

| Method (Citation) | Year | Core Technique | Dataset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mining Actionlet Ensemble (Wang et al.) [7] | 2012 | Actionlet ensemble model on skeleton joints; pairwise skeleton features and hierarchical classifiers | MSRAction3D |
| Histogram of 3D Joints (Xia et al.) [8] | 2012 | HOJ3D descriptor from 3D skeleton joint histograms; view-invariant posture representation | MSRAction3D, UTKinect-Action3D |
| Depth Motion Maps (Yang et al.) [9] | 2012 | DMM representation by stacking motion energy of depth maps on orthogonal planes; HOG extraction from DMM | MSRAction3D |
| Range-Sample Depth Feature (Lu et al.) [10] | 2014 | Binary range-sample features based on depth values; τ-tests for invariance to scale and viewpoint | MSRAction3D, Cornell Activity, UCF Kinect |
| EigenJoints (Yang et al.) [11] | 2014 | EigenJoints from 3D skeleton joints; Accumulated Motion Energy (AME) for frame filtering; NBNN classifier | MSRAction3D, Cornell Activity, UCF Kinect |
| Hierarchical Recurrent Neural Network for Skeleton (Du et al.) [12] | 2015 | End-to-end hierarchical RNN processing body, trunk, and limbs separately; LSTM cells for temporal modeling | MSRAction3D, NTU RGB+D |
| Skeleton-based CNN (Du et al.) [13] | 2015 | CNN framework for skeleton data; spatial-temporal convolution of joint positions and motion | MSRAction3D, NTU RGB+D |
| Depth information CNN fusion (Wang et al.) [14] | 2016 | Multi-modal fusion of skeleton and depth data; separate streams for depth and skeleton with SVM fusion | MSRAction3D, N-UCLA Multiview Action3D |

| Method (Citation) | Year | Core Technique | Dataset |
|---|---|---|---|

| Learning Spatiotemporal Features with 3D CNNs - C3D (Tran et al.) [16] | 2014 | 3D ConvNets with small 3x3x3 kernels; homogeneous architecture; trained on large supervised video dataset | UCF101, HMDB51, Sports-1M, ActivityNet |
| Two-Stream Convolutional Networks (Simonyan & Zisserman) [17] | 2014 | Dual CNN streams for spatial (RGB) and temporal (optical flow) information; late fusion of two networks | UCF101, HMDB51 |

| Temporal Segment Networks (Wang et al.) [20] | 2016 | Segment-based sampling and aggregation for long-range temporal modeling; multi-stream fusion (RGB and optical flow) | UCF101, HMDB51, THUMOS14, ActivityNet |

| Temporal Convolutional Networks (Lea et al.) [17] | 2017 | Temporal convolutions with dilated kernels; hierarchical temporal structure for action recognition and segmentation | UCF101, HMDB51 |
| ECO: Efficient Convolutional Network (Zolfaghari et al.) [24] | 2018 | Efficient architecture combining 3D and 2D convolutions; sparse sampling of frames for computational efficiency | UCF101, HMDB51, ActivityNet, Something-Something |

## Qualitative Comparison

### Table 6: Advantages and Disadvantages of Various Techniques

| Technique Category | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Hand-Crafted Motion Features | • Well-understood mathematical foundations; • Computationally efficient with traditional classifiers; • Effective on small-scale datasets; • Robust to certain variations (lighting, scale); • Interpretable features facilitating domain understanding; • Dense Trajectories achieve competitive performance on benchmark datasets | • Manual feature engineering required; • Limited adaptability to new domains; • Sensitive to camera motion and occlusions; • Require careful parameter tuning; • Cannot capture high-level semantic information effectively; • Performance plateaus on complex action datasets; • Optical flow computation can be time-consuming |
| Depth Information-Based Methods | • Direct access to 3D geometric information; • Robust to appearance variations and lighting changes; • Natural skeleton representation enables joint-level modeling; • Effective for human pose-based actions; • Less sensitive to clothing and appearance variations; • Enables view-invariant representations through 3D coordinates; • Actionlet ensemble captures intra-class variance | • Requires specialized depth sensors (Kinect); • Limited by depth sensor range and noise; • Skeleton extraction can be error-prone; • Fewer large-scale datasets available compared to RGB; • Performance depends heavily on skeleton quality; • Less effective for fine-grained hand gestures and subtle motions; • Depth data cannot capture texture information; • RGB-D fusion may not always improve results over RGB-alone |
| Deep Learning-Based Methods | • Automatic feature learning eliminating manual engineering; • Superior performance on large-scale datasets; • Two-stream networks effectively model spatial-temporal information; • 3D CNNs naturally capture motion in spatiotemporal volume; • Transfer learning enables learning with limited data; • Inflated 3D ConvNets (I3D) achieve state-of-the-art accuracy; • End-to-end trainable architectures; • Scalable to very large datasets (Kinetics); • Able to capture complex high-level action semantics | • Requires large annotated datasets for training from scratch; • Computationally expensive training and inference; • Significant memory requirements; • Poor generalization to unseen action classes without fine-tuning; • Difficult to interpret learned representations; • Sensitive to hyperparameter choices; • Requires careful initialization and regularization; • Two-stream networks need pre-computed optical flow increasing computational cost; • May overfit on small-scale datasets without proper regularization; • Inference latency problematic for real-time applications on resource-constrained devices |

## Key Observations

1. **Evolution Over Time**: The field has transitioned from hand-crafted features (2005-2013) through hybrid approaches combining traditional and deep learning methods (2014-2015) to predominantly deep learning-based methods (2016-2019).

2. **Benchmark Performance**: Improved Trajectories (2013) achieved 92.3% on UCF101 and 66.2% on HMDB51. Two-stream approaches (2014) reached 88.0% on UCF101, while I3D (2017) achieved state-of-the-art 98.0% on UCF101 and 80.9% on HMDB51.

3. **Depth-Based Approaches**: Most effective on MSRAction3D dataset (85.8%-91.9% accuracy), with Mining Actionlet Ensemble reaching 91.9%. These methods perform well on skeleton-based actions but less competitive on RGB action recognition.

4. **Deep Learning Dominance**: I3D and ECO represent the state-of-the-art in 2017-2018, with I3D achieving exceptional performance through 2D-to-3D convolution inflation and large-scale Kinetics pre-training.
