A Comprehensive Review of Video Activity
Recognition Techniques up to Mid-2019
Technical Survey of Hand-Crafted Motion Features
The era of hand-crafted motion features represents a foundational period in video activity
recognition, characterized by explicit feature engineering guided by human expertise in computer
vision. Before the advent of powerful deep learning models, researchers developed sophisticated
descriptors designed to capture essential spatio-temporal cues such as shape, appearance, and motion
dynamics. These methods typically involved three main stages: feature extraction from video frames
or sequences, encoding these local descriptors into a global representation for the entire video, and
finally, classification using discriminative models like Support Vector Machines (SVMs) or Hidden
Markov Models (HMMs). The core technical approach was to break down complex actions into
simpler components—either as space-time volumes or collections of local trajectories—and describe
them using histograms of oriented gradients, optical flow, or other statistical measures. While
computationally intensive and often requiring high-dimensional feature vectors, these methods were
highly effective in controlled environments and laid the groundwork for understanding the
fundamental challenges of action recognition, such as viewpoint variation and intra-class similarity.
Their performance on increasingly complex datasets served as a critical benchmark against which
later data-driven approaches would be measured.

Technical Survey of Depth Information-Based Methods
Depth information-based methods emerged as a powerful alternative to traditional RGB-only
approaches, leveraging data from depth sensors like Microsoft Kinect to overcome significant
limitations such as sensitivity to illumination changes and difficulties in extracting precise human
                                                      3 47
silhouettes from cluttered backgrounds . The core technical approach in this category revolves
around two primary modalities: processing the raw depth maps directly or analyzing the extracted
skeletal joint data. For depth map processing, a common strategy is to convert the sequence of 3D
volumetric data into more manageable 2D-like representations, such as Depth Motion Maps
(DMMs), which accumulate global activity over time and can then be analyzed with standard texture
                                                                                                                                   23
descriptors like Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) or Local Binary Patterns (LBP) . More
advanced techniques compute geometric properties directly from the 4D spatio-temporal volume,
such as the Histogram of Oriented 4D Normals (HON4D), which captures joint shape-motion cues
                                                                                      3 26
by considering surface normals in four dimensions (x, y, z, t) . Another prominent approach
involves skeleton-based analysis, where the 3D coordinates of human joints are tracked over time.
Features are then derived from these joint trajectories, including methods like EigenJoints, which
encode posture, motion, and offset information, or Histograms of 3D Joint Locations (HOJ3D),
                                                                                     3 26
which represent body poses as histograms of joint positions . These methods benefit from
providing richer structural information than 2D images alone but face their own challenges, such as
the lack of texture on depth maps, which complicates the use of local differential operators, and
                                                                             67
potential instability due to sensor noise and occlusions .

Technical Survey of Deep Learning-Based Methods
Deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), catalyzed a paradigm shift in
video activity recognition by automating the process of feature extraction and enabling the learning
                                                                                46 47
of highly discriminative spatio-temporal representations directly from data . This marked a
departure from manually designing features and instead focused on architecting networks capable of
processing video data effectively. A key innovation was the development of the two-stream CNN
architecture by Simonyan and Zisserman in 2014, which utilized one stream to process spatial
information from individual RGB frames and another stream to analyze temporal information from
                          10 24
stacked optical flow fields . By training these streams separately and fusing their outputs at the
end, this model successfully combined appearance and motion cues, achieving performance
                                                             10
competitive with the best hand-crafted methods at the time . Subsequent advancements focused on
improving upon this foundation. Tran et al. introduced C3D, a 3D CNN that applies 3D
convolutions across space and time to learn spatio-temporal features in a single pass, though it was
                           24
computationally expensive . A more efficient and powerful approach was the Inflated 3D ConvNet
(I3D) proposed by Carreira and Zisserman, which cleverly inflated pre-trained 2D ImageNet filters
into 3D kernels, allowing for effective knowledge transfer from image recognition and significantly
                                                                    13 15
boosting performance when combined with large-scale pre-training . To address long-range
temporal dependencies, architectures like Long-term Recurrent Convolutional Networks (LRCN)
integrated LSTMs with CNNs, while Temporal Segment Networks (TSN) adopted a sparse sampling
                                                                                        17 24
strategy across video segments to efficiently model global temporal structures                  . Further
innovations included Trajectory-Pooled Deep-Convolutional Descriptors (TDD), which
incorporated trajectory constraints from improved Dense Trajectories into deep feature pooling,
                                                            36
demonstrating the power of hybrid models . The success of these deep learning methods was
critically dependent on the availability of large-scale datasets like Sports-1M and, most importantly,
Kinetics, which enabled robust pre-training and established a clear dominance over previous
                                            15 19
techniques on challenging benchmarks                .

Performance Comparison on Benchmark Datasets
The evaluation of video activity recognition methods is intrinsically linked to the benchmark datasets
used, which serve as standardized platforms for measuring progress and comparing algorithmic
efficacy. The landscape of these datasets reflects the increasing complexity and realism of real-world
scenarios. Initially, datasets like Weizmann and KTH featured simple backgrounds, fixed cameras,
and well-defined actions, leading to exceptionally high accuracies for both hand-crafted and early
                        9 26
deep learning models . However, the true challenge emerged with datasets like HMDB51 and
UCF101, which contain clips sourced from movies and YouTube, respectively, introducing
                                                                                  28 47
significant variability in camera motion, lighting, viewpoints, and object scale . Performance on
these datasets became the definitive measure of a method's robustness and generalization capability.
The transition to deep learning was dramatically accelerated by the introduction of large-scale
                                                         15 24
datasets like Sports-1M and, most notably, Kinetics . Pre-training deep models on these massive
collections of diverse human actions allowed them to learn powerful, generalizable spatio-temporal
features, which could then be fine-tuned for specific tasks, leading to a qualitative leap in
                                          15 19
performance on smaller benchmarks . This co-evolution of datasets and algorithms highlights a
central theme: progress in the field has been driven by progressively more challenging data that
forces the development of more robust and expressive models.

Qualitative Analysis of Methodological Trade-offs
The progression in video activity recognition technology reveals a series of fundamental trade-offs
between different methodological paradigms. The choice between hand-crafted features, depth
information, and deep learning models is not merely a matter of academic interest but a practical
decision dictated by the specific requirements of an application, including available data,
computational resources, and environmental conditions. Hand-crafted methods offered the
advantage of interpretability and did not require the vast amounts of labeled data that would later
become a hallmark of deep learning; however, their effectiveness was often limited to the specific
                                                                                                             67
conditions under which they were designed, showing fragility in the face of real-world variations .
Depth-based methods carved out a unique niche by providing robustness to illumination changes
and enabling precise 3D skeleton extraction, making them particularly valuable in controlled indoor
settings; yet, they relied on specialized hardware and struggled with issues like sensor noise and
           3 19
occlusions . Deep learning-based methods ultimately represented the most powerful paradigm,
eliminating the need for manual feature design and automatically learning highly discriminative
representations that achieved state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of challenging
benchmarks; this power came at the cost of requiring massive datasets for training and posing
                                                                                                         6 7 19
significant computational demands, alongside the "black box" nature of many of these models .
Understanding these inherent advantages and disadvantages is crucial for navigating the complexities
of modern action recognition systems.
