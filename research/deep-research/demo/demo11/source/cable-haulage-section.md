# The Merrick Brae cable-haulage section

The steepest section of the Port Falkirk tramway is the Merrick Brae
incline: 1,260 yards at a ruling gradient of 1 in 9.2, the steepest
street-tramway grade in Scotland at the time of opening. The council
worked the incline by endless-cable haulage rather than horses.

The engineer of the cable section was Amelia Voss of the Glasgow firm
Voss & Stainer, who held the resident-engineer post for the section
from March 1895 until the conversion of 1907 — the first woman to hold
a resident-engineer appointment on a British tramway.

The cable plant stood at the Braehead depot: a 350 hp Corliss engine
driving an endless cable at a running speed of 8 mph, with the cable
carried in a conduit between the rails. After a shearing incident in
January 1897 — a cable splice parted under an overloaded two-car train
— the council replaced the cable with a slot-conduit traction system
in 1898, at a cost of £16,700.

The conduit, in turn, was abandoned when the whole tramway was
electrified in 1907.
