# The Port Falkirk Tramways Act 1893

The Port Falkirk Tramways Act received royal assent on 14 August 1893
(chapter cxlvii of that session's local acts). The Act authorised the
Burgh of Port Falkirk to construct and work 6.2 miles of street
tramway: 4.75 miles of single track and 1.45 miles of double track,
the double sections being the harbour approach, the High Street
between the Cross and the parish church, and the Merrick Brae
incline.

The construction contract was let in May 1894 to Dunsmuir & Croft of
Leith, at a contract sum of £214,000. The works comprised the track,
the Braehead depot, the Merrick Viaduct approach, and the cable plant
for the Brae incline.

A works dispute arose in 1895. The town council withheld £31,000 in
stage payments over the alignment of the Merrick Viaduct approach,
following the council's surveyor John Hedley's objection to the
girders' alignment across the Viaduct. The dispute went to arbitration
in November 1895; the arbiter, Sheriff Kenneth Royston, awarded
Dunsmuir & Croft an additional £9,400 over the contract sum.

The final construction cost to the council was therefore £223,400 —
the £214,000 contract sum plus the £9,400 arbitration award — with a
further £12,000 spent by the council directly on street widening
outside the contractor's scope. The tramway opened on 2 March 1896;
the first car left the harbour terminus at 06:10.
