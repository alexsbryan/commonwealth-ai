# The 1906-1907 electrification

The decision to electrify was taken in stages. The council's electric
traction committee reported on 21 October 1904, recommending
overhead catenary at 550 V DC; the council approved the report on
7 December 1904, and the enabling order under the Light Railways
procedure was obtained in 1905.

The conversion contract was signed on 11 June 1906 with the Falkirk
Electrical Construction Company, at a price of £88,500 — covering the
overhead equipment, the Braehead power station (two 500 kW
generating sets), and the conversion of the rolling stock. The slot
conduit on the Merrick Brae was abandoned as part of the contract.

The conversion was completed on 1 April 1907. The last cable-hauled
car ran on 31 March 1907 at 23:40. Electrified services began the
following morning.

Ridership in 1907, the first full year of electric working, was
5,912,044 passengers — an increase of 21 per cent over 1906's
4,883,947. The council attributed the increase to the faster service
and the through running that replaced the brake-change at the depot.
