Episodes BannerHealth.com Better Me Teach Me Advise Me Inspire Me By Stephanie Thurrott , Contributing Writer August 13, 2025 Teach Me Understanding and Coping With the Stages of Parkinson’s Disease When you think of Parkinson&rsquo;s disease, it&rsquo;s common to think about the movement problems it causes, like tremors or stiffness. But this condition affects the brain and nervous system, and signs of Parkinson&rsquo;s disease can include changes in mood, sleep, digestion and memory. Over time, symptoms usually get worse, but the disease looks different for everyone. For people with Parkinson&rsquo;s disease and their loved ones, understanding how it may progress can help you plan ahead, get support and improve quality of life. What is Parkinson&rsquo;s disease? &ldquo;Parkinson&rsquo;s is a neurological illness caused when nerve cells in the nervous system break down,&rdquo; said David Shprecher, DO, a neurologist specializing in movement disorders with Banner Sun Health Research Institute.
Those nerve cells stop working properly or die. These cells help make dopamine, which is a chemical that plays a big role in movement. People who are developing Parkinson&rsquo;s begin to notice changes in how they move or feel. Common symptoms of Parkinson&rsquo;s disease include: Shaking, called tremors, often in one hand Stiff muscles Trouble with balance or walking Changes in posture or facial expressions There&rsquo;s no cure yet but there are ways to manage symptoms and stay active. What are early signs and symptoms of Parkinson&rsquo;s disease?
&ldquo;Parkinson&rsquo;s is not just a movement disorder,&rdquo; said Dr. Shprecher. Many people have other symptoms that start years before they notice tremors. These can include: Constipation Loss of smell Trouble sleeping, especially acting out dreams in REM sleep &mdash; REM sleep behavior disorder is a strong risk factor for developing Parkinson&rsquo;s and dementia later in life Other common symptoms of Parkinson&rsquo;s disease include: Drooling or dry mouth Low blood pressure when standing up Voice changes Depression and anxiety Hallucinations or memory changes &ldquo;The Michael J. Fox Foundation is supporting an international study designed to follow people with these possible early symptoms to learn more about how Parkinson&rsquo;s starts and how to stop it,&rdquo; Dr. Shprecher said.
It&rsquo;s important to see a neurologist familiar with Parkinson&rsquo;s disease if you notice any signs. A neurologist can track your symptoms, recommend medications and watch for side effects that may impact your quality of life. &ldquo;A symptom tracker listing which of the motor and non-motor symptoms you have and their impact on your everyday life can be helpful in preparing for visits with your neurologist. With a few important steps, people often can continue to live well despite their Parkinson&rsquo;s for a long time,&rdquo; Dr. Shprecher said.
&ldquo;Because changes in and side effects of your other medications can affect your Parkinson&rsquo;s symptoms, you should remind each member of your health care team to send a copy of your clinic note after every appointment,&rdquo; Dr. Shprecher said.
How does Parkinson&rsquo;s progress over time?
&ldquo;Parkinson&rsquo;s is a progressive disease, which means it gets worse over time. There&rsquo;s not currently a cure,&rdquo; Dr. Shprecher said. But not everyone has the same experience. Some people have mostly movement-related symptoms, while others may notice changes in mood, sleep or digestion first.
