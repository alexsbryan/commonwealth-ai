As large language models (LLMs) move deeper into enterprise workflows, the need for seamless, scalable integration has become clear. Two emerging protocols—Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Agent2Agent (A2A)—address this challenge from different angles. Both are designed to reduce complexity, streamline operations, and support smarter automation.
While MCP and A2A are often thought of as competing protocols, they share similarities and are often used together to great effect. For some use cases, sticking to one or the other is ideal, but many organizations will unlock the most value by utilizing both.
This guide will cover:
What MCP is and how it works
What A2A is and how it works

How to choose between or combine these protocols
What is MCP?
MCP is an open-source protocol that standardizes how LLMs such as ChatGPT or Claude connect with data sources and tools. Without MCP, connecting an LLM with Google, GitHub, and other external sources often requires several unique application programming interfaces (APIs). With MCP, you can leverage one protocol across most use cases.
Anthropic developed MCP to address issues faced by developers and end users related to this complexity. For developers, the core issue is the N×M problem, where N is the number of LLMs and M is the number of systems they need to integrate with.
