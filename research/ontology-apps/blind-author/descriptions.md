# Blind-author domain descriptions

One paragraph per test system, in the voice of the person who owns the material. Written from each
source's public description and general knowledge only. Frozen by the operator before any interview;
a blind author receives exactly one section.

## crm-ward

I'm Kim Ward. I work on Enron's gas trading floor in Houston as a marketer, and this is my mailbox from
about 2000 into 2001, the copy that was released publicly with the rest of the Enron email after the
company failed. Most of it is my working mail: back and forth with the customers I sell natural gas to,
mostly utilities, municipalities and industrial users, with brokers, and with people inside Enron in
pricing, credit, contracts, scheduling and legal. A lot of it is about deals: someone wants gas somewhere
for some period, and we work out the terms over a string of messages and phone calls I only half record
in mail. Threads run for weeks, get forwarded, and mention
the same deal under different words. There is also plenty of noise, like newsletters, company
announcements and personal notes. What I want kept track of is my book of business: which deals I have
with whom, where each one stands and how that changed over time, what was agreed, and what I still owe
somebody.

## uv-support

I help maintain uv, the Python package and project manager that Astral builds in Rust, and this is our
public issue tracker on GitHub at astral-sh/uv. It holds the issues people open, the comments on them,
and what we as maintainers do with them: label, assign, link to pull requests, close, reopen, mark as
duplicates. People come in with crashes, wrong resolutions, slow installs, platform quirks, questions
about how a command should behave, and feature requests; many report the same underlying problem in
different words, often across different uv versions, operating systems and Python versions. A report can
start as a question and turn out to be a bug, or start as a bug and turn out to be a misunderstanding.
What I want kept track of is the real problems behind the reports: which issues are about the same thing,
what each one actually is, what versions and environments it shows up in, where it stands now and how it
got there, and what we decided or shipped about it.

## GVC

I put this collection together as a researcher studying how news reports events. It is the Gun Violence
Corpus, English-language news articles about gun violence incidents in the United States, gathered
around incidents listed in a public gun violence incident database, so that many articles describe the
same incident. Local papers, TV station sites and wire stories each tell it their own way: one gives the
shooter's name and another only "a man", one says two were hurt and a later one says one of them died,
one names the street and another only the city. The articles were written over days or weeks as police
released details, so early reports and later ones disagree. What I want kept track of is the incidents
themselves rather than the articles: which articles are about the same incident, what happened in it,
where and when, who was involved and what happened to each of them, and which source said what,
especially where sources disagree or a later report corrects an earlier one.
